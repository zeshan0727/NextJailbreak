ScreenRecordingMusic 1.1.0 Expands Recording Support to iOS 26 and Rootless Setups

ScreenRecordingMusic has received a substantial 1.1.0 update aimed at newer jailbreak environments and more reliable screen recording. The project’s official changelog and signed Git commit list support for iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, and 26 on rootless and roothide setups, along with fixes for muted Music playback, lock-screen recording interruptions, Control Center state synchronization, and recording persistence after a respring. The official depiction lists compatibility from iOS 12 through 26 across rootful, rootless, and roothide environments, while the package stanza supplied for the repository still identifies version 1.0.1. Users should therefore confirm that their package manager is offering the intended 1.1.0 build before proceeding.

Key points

  • ScreenRecordingMusic 1.1.0 adds explicit support for iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, and 26 in rootless and roothide environments.
  • The update addresses the stock Music app being muted during screen recording and improves recording continuity when the device locks or the screen turns off.
  • On iOS 14 and later, the changelog says recording can continue after a respring and that Control Center can reflect the correct recording state.
  • The official depiction lists iOS 12 through 26 compatibility across rootful, rootless, and roothide setups, with mobilesubstrate listed as a dependency.
  • The supplied package stanza still reports version 1.0.1, so users should verify the displayed version and source before installing.

A major update for an older recording tweak

ScreenRecordingMusic is designed to let iPhone and iPad users record the display while retaining music played through Apple’s stock Music app. The project’s original description is concise—“Screen recording is available anywhere”—but version 1.1.0 represents a much broader change than a routine maintenance release.

The official 1.1.0 changelog describes new operating-system and jailbreak-environment support as well as several behavioral fixes. The update specifically names iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, and 26 for rootless and roothide setups. The project’s current depiction presents the broader compatibility range as iOS 12 through 26 and lists rootful, rootless, and roothide environments. That distinction matters: the new release expands the explicitly named newer-platform support, while the package’s baseline remains tied to firmware 12.0 or later.

  • Project: ScreenRecordingMusic
  • Author and maintainer: ichitaso
  • New release described in the official changelog: 1.1.0
  • Package identifier: com.ichitaso.srmusic
  • Dependency listed by the official package metadata: mobilesubstrate

Music playback is no longer intended to be muted during recording

A central change in 1.1.0 is the fix for the Music app being muted when screen recording starts. The official changelog describes this as applying across iOS 12 through 26, while the depiction summarizes the behavior as recording the screen together with music playing in the stock Music app.

The source changes show that the tweak now observes recording-state changes and adjusts the handling of the Music player’s mute state during an active recording. That implementation detail explains the purpose of the update, but it should not be interpreted as a guarantee for every audio app: the official description specifically refers to Apple’s stock Music app, not third-party players or every possible audio source.

The previous 1.0.1 release addressed a different audio behavior: its changelog says it fixed sound playing when the video was set to silent. Users upgrading from 1.0.1 should therefore distinguish the older silent-video correction from the newer screen-recording Music behavior in 1.1.0.

  • 1.1.0 fixes the Music app being muted during screen recording.
  • The official description refers specifically to music played in the stock Music app.
  • Version 1.0.1 previously addressed sound playing when video was set to silent.

Lock-screen and display-off recording improvements

The update also targets interruptions caused by device locking or the display turning off. According to the official changelog, 1.1.0 keeps recording when the device is locked or the screen turns off on iOS 14 and later. The depiction presents the same behavior as a core feature.

This is separate from the ability to begin or stop a recording from the lock screen. The project says the Control Center record button can be used on the lock screen without a passcode, and the 1.1.0 changelog specifically identifies a fix for the Control Center record button not working there on iOS 14 and later.

These claims are version- and platform-sensitive. The source explicitly attaches the lock-screen recording and Control Center lock-screen behavior to iOS 14 or later, so users on earlier versions should not assume that every lock-screen feature applies in the same way.

  • Recording is intended to continue when the device locks or the screen turns off on iOS 14 and later.
  • The lock-screen Control Center record button is described as usable without a passcode.
  • The changelog identifies a fix for the lock-screen Control Center record button on iOS 14 and later.

Respring persistence and Control Center state synchronization

Another important 1.1.0 change concerns resprings. The official release notes say recording keeps running after a respring and that Control Center shows the correct recording state afterward. The depiction adds that users can still stop the recording from the status bar or Control Center on iOS 14 and later.

The accompanying source changes indicate that the tweak publishes recording state and synchronizes recorder and Control Center objects after the relevant processes reconnect. It also includes handling for newer Control Center behavior, including iOS 18 and later references in the source. Those implementation details support why the release notes mention state synchronization, but the user-facing claim remains the one provided by the project: recording persistence and correct Control Center state after a respring.

A respring is not the same as a full reboot or jailbreak restoration. The official material discusses respring behavior specifically and does not promise that an active recording will survive every type of restart, shutdown, or environment change.

  • The release notes say an active recording can continue after a respring.
  • Control Center is intended to show the correct recording state after that respring.
  • The source does not establish equivalent persistence through a full reboot or other restart types.

Compatibility, packaging, and an important version discrepancy

The official depiction lists ScreenRecordingMusic as compatible with iOS 12 to 26 and with rootful, rootless, and roothide environments. The package metadata lists a firmware pre-dependency of 12.0 or later and a dependency on mobilesubstrate. The 1.1.0 build configuration also distinguishes rootless and roothide targets from the rootful target, while the changelog notes that the rootful package uses the older arm64e ABI for iOS 12 and 13.

However, the supplied official repository package stanza still reports Version: 1.0.1 and retains older compatibility tags for iOS 12, 13, and 14. By contrast, the official depiction changelog, repository commit message, and updated control-file patch describe version 1.1.0 and iOS 26 compatibility. Because these source records do not agree, readers should not rely on the package stanza alone when identifying the newest build.

Before installing, check the version displayed by the package manager, confirm that it is the official ScreenRecordingMusic package, and ensure that the selected package matches the jailbreak environment in use. The project lists no settings panel and describes the tweak as requiring no configuration, but that does not remove the need to confirm architecture, firmware, substrate support, and package scheme compatibility.

  • The depiction states iOS 12–26 compatibility across rootful, rootless, and roothide.
  • The package metadata lists firmware 12.0 or later and mobilesubstrate.
  • The 1.1.0 build notes distinguish rootless, roothide, and rootful packaging targets.
  • The supplied package stanza reports 1.0.1, creating a version discrepancy that users should resolve before installation.

License change and what users should verify

ScreenRecordingMusic was released under the MIT License through version 1.0.1. The project README and 1.1.0 changelog state that the license changed to the GNU Affero General Public License version 3.0 beginning with 1.1.0. That is relevant for developers who modify, redistribute, or integrate the project, and it is also a useful indicator that the repository’s licensing files changed alongside the functional update.

The official depiction identifies the project as free and says there are no settings to configure. It also lists tested device and jailbreak combinations, including examples covering iOS 12.4 through iOS 18.6 and an iOS 26.0.1 device. Those entries are claims published by the project; they are not an independent guarantee for every device, jailbreak, or future build.

Readers should verify the exact iOS version, jailbreak type, package version, dependency availability, and whether the package manager is presenting the rootful, rootless, or roothide variant appropriate to the device. It is also sensible to review the project’s current release notes for any changes after the supplied 1.1.0 commit.

  • Versions through 1.0.1 used the MIT License.
  • Version 1.1.0 is licensed under AGPL-3.0 according to the project README and changelog.
  • The depiction lists project-reported tested device and jailbreak combinations, but coverage is not universal.

Frequently asked questions

What does ScreenRecordingMusic 1.1.0 do?

It is a jailbreak tweak intended to record the screen while retaining music played through Apple’s stock Music app. The official 1.1.0 notes also describe lock-screen recording improvements, Control Center fixes, and recording persistence after a respring.

Which iOS versions are listed as compatible?

The official depiction lists iOS 12 through 26, with rootful, rootless, and roothide support. The 1.1.0 changelog specifically adds support for iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, and 26 on rootless and roothide environments. Users should still match the package variant to their jailbreak setup.

Does it work with every music app?

The supplied official material specifically refers to the stock Music app. It does not establish equivalent support for third-party music applications, so users should not generalize the claim beyond Apple’s Music app.

Can recording continue after the screen locks?

The project says recording continues when the device is locked or the screen turns off on iOS 14 and later. The same iOS 14-or-later qualification applies to the documented lock-screen Control Center behavior.

Will recording survive a respring?

The 1.1.0 changelog and depiction say that recording can continue after a respring and that Control Center synchronizes its displayed state. The source material does not promise that recordings survive every kind of reboot or shutdown.

Before installing: confirm the current package version, compatibility and dependencies at the original developer/repository source for your exact device and jailbreak.
