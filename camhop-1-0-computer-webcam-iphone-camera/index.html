CamHop 1.0.0 Turns a Computer Webcam into an iPhone Camera

CamHop replaces the iPhone or iPad camera feed in selected apps with a live webcam from a Mac, PC, tablet or another phone. The first release works through a browser tab over local Wi-Fi, can carry the computer microphone, and gives you per-camera, per-app and emergency restore controls.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 29, 2026 · Version, compatibility, price, limitations and visuals checked against the official Havoc listing

Compatibility has two separate parts. Havoc lists CamHop for rootless iOS/iPadOS 15.0–26.0.1, but that package range does not mean every device on every listed firmware has a public jailbreak. The listing also does not document RootHide support.

What CamHop 1.0.0 actually does

CamHop takes a live camera and microphone selected in a computer browser, sends them directly to the jailbroken device, and exposes that stream as the device camera or microphone. You can replace the front camera, back camera or both, while excluding apps that should keep Apple’s camera feed.

The browser side needs no CamHop installer. The official listing names Chrome, Edge and Safari, and says a tablet or another phone can also supply its camera through the browser. Video is available at 1080p, 720p or 480p, with fit, fill, stretch, rotation, mirror, zoom and pan controls. The microphone can replace or mix with the iPhone microphone in selected apps.

How to connect a computer webcam

  1. Install CamHop from its official Havoc package on a compatible rootless jailbreak and respring if your package manager requests it.
  2. Open the CamHop app and enable Desktop Camera. Enable Desktop Microphone only if you also want computer audio.
  3. Send the displayed link to the computer, scan the QR code, or use AirDrop. Open it in Chrome, Edge or Safari.
  4. Allow the browser to access the webcam and microphone you intend to use, then enter the six-digit PIN shown on the iPhone or iPad.
  5. Preview the feed before opening the target iOS app. Choose whether CamHop replaces the front camera, back camera or both, and configure any app exclusions.

Privacy and network behavior

AADiOS says picture and sound travel directly from the browser to the iPhone or iPad with encryption and never pass through a server. The device hosts the connection, so internet access is not required. CamHop allows one computer at a time, uses a PIN for pairing, and does not require an account or tracking.

“No internet required” does not mean “no network required.” The browser and iOS device still need to reach each other locally. Guest Wi-Fi and networks with client isolation may block peer-to-peer traffic even when both devices show the same Wi-Fi name.

Practical troubleshooting

  1. No connection: put both devices on the same local network, avoid guest Wi-Fi/client isolation, regenerate the PIN, and reopen the link.
  2. Black or frozen picture: grant camera permission to the browser tab, select the correct webcam there, and close other desktop apps that have exclusive camera access.
  3. Choppy video: reduce the stream from 1080p to 720p or 480p and test closer to the access point. Watch the live speed and frame-rate indicators before blaming the iOS app.
  4. Wrong camera inside one app: check whether CamHop is replacing front, back or both cameras and whether that app appears in the exclusion list.
  5. No or doubled audio: verify Desktop Microphone, choose the intended browser microphone, then decide whether the iPhone microphone should be replaced or mixed.
  6. Wrong orientation or crop: use rotate, mirror, fit/fill/stretch, zoom and pan in CamHop’s preview before reopening the target app.
  7. Need the stock camera immediately: use CamHop’s Control Center control, floating button or both volume buttons together.

If the entire device enters a tweak crash loop, use the Dopamine bad-tweak recovery guide. For repeatable app crashes, capture useful evidence with the KrashKop crash-log guide before contacting the developer.

Who CamHop is for

The clearest use case is someone who wants a higher-quality or differently positioned webcam inside an iOS camera-using app without installing a desktop companion. It is also useful when a second phone or tablet is the live camera source. It is not the right tool for injecting prerecorded clips, screen captures, slides or overlays, because the developer explicitly limits CamHop to live cameras.

Frequently asked questions

Does CamHop need a computer app?

No. The official workflow runs in a Chrome, Edge or Safari browser tab.

Does CamHop support RootHide?

The listing says rootless, but it does not claim RootHide support. Do not treat those terms as interchangeable.

Can it send a video file or screen recording as the camera?

No. CamHop supports live cameras only, not files, photos, screen mirroring or overlays.

Can it work without internet?

Yes, according to the developer, because the device hosts the direct connection. The devices still need a working local path to each other.

How do I switch back to the iPhone camera?

Use the Control Center control, floating button or both volume buttons together, depending on the controls you enabled.
