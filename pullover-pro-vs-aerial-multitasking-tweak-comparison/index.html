PullOver Pro 3.0.1 vs Aerial 5.1 vs PanoramicView 1.1.0: Which Multitasking Style Fits?

Compare PullOver Pro 3.0.1, Aerial 5.1 and PanoramicView 1.1.0 by multitasking model, iOS and device limits, RootHide support, price and conflict risk.

Published November 15, 2026Updated November 25, 2026By Zeeshan Barvi

Short answer: choose PullOver Pro 3.0.1 for one app floating over the app you are using, a free package, or an explicitly published RootHide build. Choose PanoramicView 1.1.0 when you want two full-size apps paired on an oversized canvas; this release adds Home-button iPhones, while iPhone Duo and iPad remain excluded. Choose Aerial 5.1 for movable, resizable Stage Manager-style windows and Split Stage.

These are three different multitasking models, not interchangeable window skins. PullOver Pro overlays one app; PanoramicView pairs two apps at full size, with one waiting at the edge; Aerial manages stages and split layouts. The right choice depends on device form factor, jailbreak environment and how you switch between apps—not only the feature list.

Choose PullOver Pro for a fast single-app overlay

Choose PullOver Pro for a fast single-app overlay

PullOver Pro’s strength is immediacy. Its handle stays near the screen edge, opens a selected app above what you are doing, and can expose recent or favorite apps. QuickSwitch lets you drag the overlay toward full screen. Version 3.0.1 specifically fixes the handle disappearing after a respring, so it is the current build to evaluate rather than the earlier 3.0 release.

It is the clearer option for RootHide users because the official package page explicitly lists both Rootless and RootHide. It also documents AltList as a dependency and points iOS 27 users to TweakSettings for preferences. Read the PullOver Pro 3.0.1 update notes for the release-specific setup and tested-device disclosures.

PanoramicView: pair two full-size apps on one oversized canvas

Updated November 25: PanoramicView 1.1.0 is the current Havoc release, published at 05:29:51 UTC. Instead of floating a smaller app over another, it keeps two apps at their normal full size on a canvas larger than the display. One app fills the screen while the other waits at the edge; tap the edge card or drag the divider to switch.

What PanoramicView 1.1.0 changes

  • Home-button iPhones can now pair apps: press Home once, then press it again and hold; choose the second app from the Home Screen or App Switcher. Press Home once to cancel, while a double press still opens App Switcher.
  • Pair from Notifications expands: when that option is enabled, tapping a Dynamic Island activity can pair its app with the app in use, matching notification-banner behavior.
  • Pair recovery is cleaner: if one paired app quits, the other fills the screen immediately; Home-button, keyboard and AssistiveTouch exits now shrink the whole pair; the update also fixes a white bar left by one App Switcher gesture.
  • Keyboard and rotation fixes: the changelog addresses high or hidden text fields, including iOS 15 messaging apps and docked Picture in Picture, plus iOS 15 full-screen video and in-app rotation problems.
  • Create a pair by swiping up from the bottom of an app and holding until “Release to keep” appears, then choose another app from the Home Screen or App Switcher.
  • Pairs work in portrait and landscape. A pair becomes one App Switcher card and is retained after a restart.
  • Save a pair to the Home Screen and open both apps with one tap. Home Screen bookmarks can also be paired, and a pair can be opened through a link or Shortcuts.
  • Landscape video in Picture in Picture can dock between the paired apps; drag-and-drop can move content across the pair. In landscape, one app can fill the screen while the other remains in Slide Over.

Havoc lists PanoramicView at $6.99, version 1.1.0, 734,168 bytes and iOS 15.0–26.0.1. The developer description now supports a regular iPhone with iOS 15 or later, explicitly including a Home-button workflow, and names Dopamine with Rootless and RootHide. iPhone Duo and iPad remain unsupported. The listing does not name palera1n or iOS 27, so do not infer either from the broad package range.

Choose it when two full-size app contexts matter more than a floating window manager. It is not the option for an iPad, iPhone Duo, iOS 27 device or a device whose only available jailbreak path is not the one the listing names.

Choose Aerial for real window management

Aerial’s central menu can open, maximize, minimize, split and close windows. Resize handles preserve or change aspect ratio, Trim Mode changes the visible crop, and Split Stage arranges applications together. Version 5.1 replaces the earlier one-third iPad split with a four-way split, fixes landscape stage reversion and a close-animation enlargement bug, and attempts to improve keyboard sizing during iPad rotation.

That breadth is why Aerial costs more and has more interaction surface to test. It is the better fit when resizing, arranging and revisiting several windows matters more than reaching one utility quickly. The Aerial 5.1 update guide covers the point release in detail.

Read compatibility labels carefully

All three are jailbreak tweaks. A package’s iOS range is not proof that a working jailbreak exists for every device and build inside that range. Check the exact model, chip, firmware build and jailbreak environment before installing.

  • PullOver Pro 3.0.1: the project lists iOS 15–27 and Rootless/RootHide. AltList is required. The iOS 17 fix remains unconfirmed on an iOS 17 phone.
  • PanoramicView 1.1.0: Havoc lists iOS 15.0–26.0.1 and Dopamine with Rootless/RootHide labels. Version 1.1.0 adds Home-button iPhones; iPhone Duo, iPad, iOS 27 and palera1n remain unlisted or excluded.
  • Aerial 5.1: Havoc metadata lists iOS 15.0–26.0.1, but the product description still says 15.0–17.7.10. It is marked Rootless; RootHide is not claimed.

If you are on iOS 27, PullOver Pro is the only one of these three whose current project page explicitly lists that major version. If you use an iPad, PanoramicView is excluded, while Aerial’s 5.1 changelog specifically mentions the iPad four-way split. In either case, confirm that your exact device and firmware have a compatible jailbreak first with the model and build compatibility pre-check.

Conflict and installation order

Aerial’s official page warns that it conflicts with unofficial PullOver-Pro or PullOver-Pro-based tweaks. The current PullOver Pro is a maintained port/rebuild of the original project, so treat the combination as a conflict risk rather than assuming both can run together. PanoramicView’s listing does not publish a conflict matrix; that absence is not proof that it is compatible with either window system.

For a clean comparison, install and evaluate one multitasking tweak at a time:

  1. Record the installed package version and save any settings you need to restore.
  2. Remove the current multitasking tweak in Sileo and respring before trying another.
  3. Install only the replacement from its official source and confirm the package manager selected the correct jailbreak environment.
  4. Test app launch, rotation, keyboard display, lock/unlock, App Switcher behavior and one respring.
  5. If SpringBoard enters Safe Mode, use Sileo in Safe Mode to remove the last installed package before re-enabling other tweaks.

Use the Sileo installation guide for repository hygiene and the bad-tweak recovery guide before experimenting with another global SpringBoard tweak.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose PullOver Pro 3.0.1 for a free, quick single-app overlay, an explicitly listed RootHide package, or the only one of these three that currently lists iOS 27.
  • Choose PanoramicView 1.1.0 for two full-size apps on a regular iPhone—including a Home-button model—if your jailbreak is the Dopamine Rootless or RootHide environment named by Havoc. Do not choose it for iPhone Duo, iPad or iOS 27.
  • Choose Aerial 5.1 for movable/resizable stages, Split Stage and its iPad four-way split. It is the Rootless option here, but verify the seller’s inconsistent iOS range before paying.
  • Do not combine Aerial with PullOver Pro without explicit maintainer confirmation. PanoramicView has no published conflict matrix, so test it separately rather than assuming it can coexist with another global multitasking tweak.
  • Choose none yet if your exact device, firmware and jailbreak path are not supported. A tweak’s package range does not create a jailbreak for an unsupported device.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between PanoramicView and PullOver Pro?

PullOver Pro places one selected app over the app you are using. PanoramicView pairs two full-size apps on an oversized canvas and switches between them using an edge card or divider.

Does PanoramicView support Home-button iPhones or iPad?

Version 1.1.0 supports regular Home-button iPhones with a dedicated press-and-hold workflow. iPhone Duo and iPad remain unsupported.

Which of the three lists RootHide support?

PullOver Pro 3.0.1 publishes Rootless and RootHide packages. Havoc labels PanoramicView Rootless and RootHide and names Dopamine. Aerial is marked Rootless; its official page does not claim RootHide support.

Does PanoramicView support iOS 27?

No iOS 27 support is listed. Havoc currently lists iOS 15.0–26.0.1, and a compatible Dopamine jailbreak environment is still required.

Does Aerial support iOS 26?

Havoc metadata lists iOS 15.0–26.0.1 and the version history says iOS 26 support was added, but the product description still says iOS 15.0–17.7.10. Verify the live package-manager compatibility view before purchasing.

Can Aerial and PullOver Pro run together?

Aerial’s official page warns about conflicts with unofficial PullOver-Pro or derivative tweaks. Treat the combination as unsupported unless the maintainers confirm otherwise, and test one multitasking tweak at a time.
