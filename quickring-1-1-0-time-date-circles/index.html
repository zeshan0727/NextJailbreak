QuickRing 1.1.0 Adds Time and Date Circles, Fixes iOS 26 Safe Mode

QuickRing’s latest listed release adds time and date circles, expands its color options and addresses an iOS 26 battery-monitoring crash. Here is what the official changelog confirms, how the controls look, and what to check before adding it to your jailbreak setup.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 4, 2026 · Package released September 28, 2026

What is new in QuickRing 1.1.0?

QuickRing 1.1.0 expands moondr1’s circular status-bar interface with separate time and date displays. Havoc dates this release to September 28, 2026; it remains the current version on the listing checked October 4. Weather was already present in the initial release, so the time and date circles are the additions to focus on here.

The update also introduces Adaptive, Solid, Gradient, Mixed and Color Wheel modes. You can assign separate colors to the main control, weather, time and date, or link them together. Gradient endpoints and live positioning controls provide more flexibility when arranging the circles around your existing layout.

The stability fix matters as much as the design

The developer reports a fix for an iOS 26 Safe Mode issue caused by battery monitoring. The changelog also describes a replacement for the older clock-hiding hook and a unified approach to hiding the stock clock, Focus indicators and carrier text. These are specific changes; they do not establish compatibility with every other status-bar tweak.

If a previous QuickRing setup caused a problem, keep the first test after updating simple. Reproduce the same action that previously failed before adjusting colors or adding more circles. That gives you a clearer answer about the fix than changing several parts of the setup at the same time.

What the controls show

QuickRing combines connectivity controls with battery information, weather and privacy indicators. Its circular battery display includes a percentage, a low-charge state and a charging indicator. The official controls image below illustrates the interface and the new time/date layout.

When choosing a layout, start with the information you check most often. A time circle and one frequently used control may be more useful than displaying every option. Check the result against both a light app background and your darker wallpaper: a color combination that looks good on one screen may be harder to read on another.

Compatibility and price

Havoc lists iOS 16.0–26.0.1, rootless support and a US$2.49 price. The developer reports testing on iOS 16, 17, 18 and 26.0.1. These are the project’s stated requirements and testing claims; Next Jailbreak has not performed an on-device review. A compatible jailbreak is required, and the listing does not confirm a separate RootHide build.

A package’s supported iOS range is not a list of devices that can be jailbroken. Verify your device and existing jailbreak first, then check that the package manager offers the correct build. If your environment is RootHide, ask the developer about that setup instead of assuming the rootless label covers it.

Setup checks before daily use

  1. Open the official Havoc listing and confirm the version and requirements before purchasing or installing.
  2. After installation, use QuickRing’s preferences to enable the tweak and begin with a minimal selection of indicators.
  3. Adjust positioning and spacing gradually, checking that the information remains readable beside your notch or Dynamic Island.
  4. Test charging, your usual connectivity controls and the screens you use most. Temporarily disable overlapping status-bar customizations if you see duplicated or obscured information.

For troubleshooting, note your device model, iOS release, jailbreak environment, QuickRing version and the exact action that triggered the issue. A reproducible example is more useful than a general report that the layout looks wrong. Support details are available on the official listing.

How it differs from lilywhite

QuickRing’s appeal is its circular controls and individually configurable displays. Our lilywhite article covers another approach to status-bar customization. Compare the original screenshots before choosing a layout, and evaluate one status-bar replacement at a time.
