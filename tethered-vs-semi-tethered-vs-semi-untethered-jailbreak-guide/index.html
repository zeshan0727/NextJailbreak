Tethered vs Semi-Tethered vs Semi-Untethered Jailbreaks: What Happens After Reboot

The label tells you what the phone can boot after power loss and what you need to reactivate the jailbreak—not which iOS versions or devices are supported.

By Zeeshan BarviNovember 21, 2026
Fast answer: tethered needs a host for every patched boot; semi-tethered boots stock iOS but needs a host to jailbreak again; semi-untethered boots stock iOS and can re-jailbreak from an on-device app; untethered preserves the jailbreak through a normal reboot. None of these terms proves compatibility with your device or firmware.

Tethered: the host is part of every boot

A tethered jailbreak cannot start its patched operating system after power loss without an external host. This is the strictest meaning of “needs a computer.” It is more than needing a Mac for the first installation.

Liter8 is a clear current example. Its official README says the iPhone boots through pwn DFU with fw boot, so the Mac is required every time. Its standard supported target is the regular iPhone 11 (iPhone12,1/n104ap), not every A13 model. Its listed iOS 27 builds also do not prove that an unsigned firmware can still be restored: the workflow obtains a fresh APTicket, so signing still matters.

This is not a normal consumer recovery path. Liter8’s workflow involves a custom-firmware erase restore and has feature limitations. Use the current Liter8 status guide before considering it.

Semi-tethered: stock boots, computer reactivates jailbreak

A semi-tethered device can reboot into ordinary stock iOS without a computer. Jailbreak files may remain on storage, but tweak injection, package-manager helpers and elevated access are inactive until the host-side jailbreak process runs again.

palera1n’s current guide covers A11 and earlier devices on iOS 15 or later, with important A11 caveats. Re-jailbreaking requires repeating the host command and DFU flow. On A11 devices running iOS 16, the official guide says passcode and other SEP features cannot be used while jailbroken; a device that previously had a passcode may need an erase before it can be jailbroken. Those are device and security restrictions, not consequences shared by every semi-tethered tool.

Semi-untethered: the phone can re-jailbreak itself

A semi-untethered jailbreak also reboots into stock iOS. The difference is that an app already installed on the device can reactivate the jailbreak without a computer. Dopamine identifies itself as a rootless semi-untethered jailbreak.

The app still has to launch. A TrollStore installation is normally persistent on compatible systems; a traditionally sideloaded app may expire or need re-signing. Rebooting does not remove the jailbreak environment, but it stops its runtime components until Dopamine runs successfully again. Follow the Dopamine post-reboot guide rather than reinstalling packages at random.

Untethered: reboot persistence

An untethered jailbreak automatically returns in a jailbroken state after a normal reboot. No computer or manually launched jailbreak app is required. The word describes persistence, not exploit quality, package format or bypass strength.

Do not call a tool untethered merely because its app is permanently signed or because its files survive reboot. The defining test is whether the jailbreak itself becomes active automatically after the boot completes.

TrollStore is not a fifth jailbreak type

TrollStore permanently installs eligible apps using a CoreTrust verification flaw. Its official documentation explicitly says it is not a jailbreak. It can make a semi-untethered jailbreak app easier to keep available, but TrollStore alone does not enable system-wide tweak injection or turn an unsupported firmware into a jailbreakable one.

The official range is iOS 14.0 beta 2 through 16.6.1, iOS 16.7 RC build 20H18, and iOS 17.0. Other 16.7.x builds and iOS 17.0.1 or later are not supported by the existing CoreTrust bugs. See the IPA installer comparison for the practical differences.

What to do after an unexpected reboot

  1. Let stock iOS finish booting. On semi-tethered and semi-untethered setups, Sileo icons may remain while tweak injection is inactive.
  2. Identify the actual tool. Do not follow Dopamine steps on palera1n or a tethered custom-firmware project.
  3. Check the app or host requirement. Open Dopamine for semi-untethered; reconnect to the supported Mac/Linux workflow for palera1n; use the exact project boot flow for a tethered build.
  4. Do not update iOS as a generic fix. An update may remove the only supported firmware. Check signing and compatibility first.
  5. If re-jailbreaking loops or crashes, disable tweak injection. Then remove the most recent problematic tweak using the Dopamine crash-loop recovery guide.

Compatibility still needs a separate check

The tethering label says nothing about chip support. Dopamine’s current 3.x README lists arm64e support on iOS 15.0–17.3.1, broader A12/A13 support through iOS 18.7.1 and iOS 26.0–26.0.1, and arm64 support through iOS 18.7.1. palera1n targets checkm8-era A8–A11 devices. Liter8’s verified standard target is narrower still.

Record your exact model, chip, iOS build and installation method, then use the device and build identification guide. Package compatibility does not create a jailbreak for hardware that the tool does not support.
