lilywhite 1.0.1 Refines Its Status Bar Capsule for Jailbroken iPhones

lilywhite takes the information normally spread across an iPhone’s status bar and groups it around a capsule clock. Version 1.0.1, dated September 30, 2026 on Havoc, addresses a mobile-data layout problem and improves licence activation. Its device restrictions are worth checking before you buy.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 1, 2026 · Package updated 2026-09-30

What version 1.0.1 fixes

The official changelog says network-type text could overlap notification icons when using mobile data. Version 1.0.1 fixes that, improves licence-check reliability and makes the message shown on unregistered devices clearer. This is a refinement of the existing interface, rather than a newly announced jailbreak or compatibility expansion.

How the capsule works

The clock and Wi-Fi indicator share a capsule, with the outline representing battery charge and dots showing cellular strength. A nearby indicator covers states such as Focus, recording, silent mode or location use. Up to three notification-app icons can appear across the notch. The official screenshot above shows the intended arrangement.

The design question is whether bringing these indicators together makes them easier for you to read. Look at the battery outline and signal dots at normal phone-viewing distance, rather than judging only the enlarged promotional image. A layout can look attractive in a screenshot while requiring more attention than you want during everyday use.

Supported devices and jailbreak environment

Havoc lists iOS 16.0–26.0 and US$2.49 at publication. The developer specifies notched or Dynamic Island iPhones using a rootless Dopamine and ElleKit setup. iPads, iPod touch, iPhone SE and older non-notched iPhones are excluded. The listing does not establish a RootHide-compatible build.

Do not infer that every iPhone running a listed iOS release can be jailbroken. Check your existing jailbreak environment separately. Also avoid buying this solely because another tweak works on your phone: status-bar modifications can have narrower device requirements than general SpringBoard tweaks.

Installation and first checks

The developer’s release instructions say to open Settings → lilywhite after installation to activate it, then respring when prompted. The same announcement warns about conflicts with other status-bar tweaks.

Our suggested first check is the problem addressed by this update: view the status bar while on mobile data with notifications present. Check that the network label and app icons stay readable. Then compare the Home Screen, Lock Screen and an app you regularly use, since your wallpaper and app backgrounds may make small indicators easier or harder to distinguish.

Keep the first trial simple. If you already customize the clock, battery indicator or notification area, temporarily disable the overlapping customization before judging lilywhite. Reintroducing changes individually will make a conflict easier to identify than changing several settings at once.

Who should consider it?

This is most relevant if you want a different presentation of status information while retaining a compact layout. It is less compelling if your priority is a completely familiar arrangement, or if you rely on another status-bar modification that you are unwilling to change. The original image is especially useful here: inspect the placement before deciding whether the design suits your setup.

If activation or layout still fails, use the support options linked from the official Havoc page and describe the precise screen or action involved. Next Jailbreak has checked the source information and imagery; this article is not a claim of hands-on device testing.
