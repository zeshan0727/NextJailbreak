PS5 13.60 PSN Goes Offline — Relapse Jailbreak Enters Final Release Checks

The event the PS5 jailbreak community has been waiting for appears to have happened: users on firmware 13.60 are now reporting that PSN access has gone offline in multiple regions. At the same time, the upcoming Relapse/WebKit jailbreak is being described as being in final release checks. Here is what is confirmed, what is still pending, and what PS5 owners should avoid doing.

Breaking status at publication: PS5 firmware 13.60 is being reported offline for PSN, and final jailbreak checks are underway. The new Relapse jailbreak itself is not yet independently verified as publicly downloadable. Ignore reposted “download” links until the developer or a trusted project source publishes the official host or repository.

What just happened with PS5 firmware 13.60?

On September 29, 2026, PS5 users began reporting that firmware 13.60 could no longer connect to PlayStation Network. Multiple users in different regions confirmed the change, including reports from the United Kingdom. This matters because developers and community trackers had repeatedly pointed to the end of PSN access on 13.60 as the practical release trigger for the next public jailbreak chain.

The shutdown follows Sony's rollout of PS5 system software 14.00 earlier this month. For jailbreak users, the important distinction is that 13.60 is now an older firmware branch. The new exploit work discussed by developers targets 13.60-era systems rather than current 14.xx consoles.

Relapse is now in final release checks

Almost immediately after the 13.60 PSN reports appeared, new community posts described the jailbreak as undergoing final rechecks before public release. The upcoming browser-based chain has commonly been referred to as Relapse in PS5 scene discussions.

Recent demonstrations have shown a much faster WebKit-based entry than the long waits associated with some P2JB workflows. That is one of the biggest practical improvements expected from this release: users should no longer need to rely on the same slow game-assisted entry process for every compatible setup.

However, “final checks” is not the same thing as “download released.” At the time this article was published, we could not verify an official public Relapse repository, host URL, or final release package. That distinction is important because fake mirrors and copied payload pages tend to appear quickly around major jailbreak releases.

Which PS5 firmware versions are expected to work?

The new chain is being discussed around PS5 firmware 13.60 and lower supported firmware. Earlier public scene updates described the WebKit/kernel work as being backported across older versions, but the final compatibility matrix should come from the actual release notes rather than from social-media summaries.

If your PS5 is already on a lower jailbreak-friendly firmware, do not update to 13.60 just because this release is focused on 13.60. Lower firmware generally preserves more exploit options. The safest approach is to stay where you are until the official release documentation confirms the exact path for your version.

Do not update a waiting console to PS5 14.00

If you are preserving a PS5 specifically for homebrew or jailbreak research, updating to firmware 14.00 would move the console outside the 13.60-targeted release discussed here. Public exploit tracking currently treats the 14.xx branch separately, and the upcoming 13.60 chain should not be assumed to work there.

This is especially important because PS5 system updates cannot normally be rolled back by ordinary users. Once a console moves to a newer patched firmware, waiting for a future exploit may be the only practical option.

Kstuff Lite support is not the jailbreak itself

There has already been significant progress around payload support on newer PS5 firmware, including Kstuff-related work extending toward 13.60. That does not mean Kstuff Lite alone is the new jailbreak. A jailbreak still needs a usable entry point and exploit chain capable of reaching the environment where those payloads can run.

The Relapse/WebKit work is important because it is expected to provide that faster browser-facing entry path. Payload compatibility and jailbreak entry are related parts of the ecosystem, but they should not be treated as the same release.

Does Relapse include the PS5 hypervisor exploit?

No public release information currently confirms that the upcoming Relapse package includes a full PS5 hypervisor exploit. Recent scene discussion has treated the kernel/WebKit jailbreak chain and the newer hypervisor research as separate pieces of work.

That means users should not automatically interpret the 13.60 jailbreak release as “full hypervisor jailbreak,” full Linux support on every affected firmware, or unrestricted access to every PS5 subsystem. Those capabilities depend on additional research and payload support beyond simply obtaining a kernel jailbreak.

What should PS5 owners do right now?

  • Stay on your current lower firmware. Do not update solely to chase 13.60 compatibility.
  • Avoid PS5 14.00 if your goal is to preserve eligibility for the upcoming 13.60-era exploit chain.
  • Do not download random “Relapse” packages yet. Wait for the developer's official host, repository, or a source directly linked by the project.
  • Keep automatic updates disabled on a console intentionally preserved for jailbreak research.
  • Separate confirmed releases from payload updates. Kstuff, etaHEN and other payload projects may have their own firmware-support schedules.

Why this release matters

Firmware 13.60 represents a major expansion of the practical PS5 jailbreak range compared with the firmware versions that had reliable public entry methods earlier in the year. The biggest improvement is not simply a higher version number; it is the expected combination of a modern WebKit entry, kernel exploitation and existing payload work that has been prepared for newer firmware.

For users sitting on 12.xx or 13.xx consoles, the release could also reduce the friction of starting the jailbreak. Community demonstrations indicate the new path can execute in seconds rather than the lengthy wait associated with some P2JB setups.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kstuff Lite 1.11 the new PS5 jailbreak?

No. Kstuff is payload-side functionality. The new WebKit/kernel exploit chain is the part expected to provide the jailbreak entry on supported firmware.

Does this automatically mean full hypervisor access or Linux?

No. Kernel jailbreak progress and hypervisor exploitation are separate technical milestones. Wait for explicit release documentation before assuming hypervisor or Linux capabilities.
