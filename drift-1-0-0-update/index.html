Drift 1.1.0 Adds Dock Effects, Reflection and Floating Dock on iPhone

Drift 1.1.0 expands Antony .M’s scrollable iOS dock tweak with five new edge effects, four scroll effects, optional reflection and a position indicator. It can also bring the iPad-style Floating Dock to iPhone. The official Chariz listing still limits support to jailbroken iPhone, iPad and iPod touch devices on iOS 15.0 through 26.0.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published November 4, 2026 · Updated November 8, 2026

What changed in Drift 1.1.0

Chariz records Drift 1.1.0 as released on November 5, 2026 at 06:32:11 UTC. This is a meaningful feature update rather than a version-label refresh:

  • Five additional edge effects: Blur, Sink, Carousel, Curve, and Fade & Shrink join the original Fade and Shrink options.
  • Four scroll effects: Magnify, Spring, Tilt and Wave change how icons react while the dock moves.
  • Optional reflection: adds a mirrored treatment beneath dock icons.
  • Optional position indicator: shows where the visible section sits within a larger dock.
  • Floating Dock on iPhone: provides an iPad-style dock with recent apps that opens from inside apps with a swipe up. The developer says this option requires a respring.
  • Two fixes: a stationary dock no longer leaves the end icons covered by the fade effect, and haptic ticks now work on iPhone 6s and 6s Plus.

Who should update

Version 1.1.0 is most useful for people who already like Drift’s up-to-50-item dock but want clearer navigation or more visual feedback. The position indicator helps when the dock extends across several screens, while reflection and the new effect sets are cosmetic choices. Floating Dock is the functional addition: it makes recent apps reachable from within another app instead of only from the Home Screen.

If Drift 1.0.0 already works and you use only its basic horizontal dock, the update is not described as a security fix. Users affected by fade covering the end icons—or missing edge haptics on an iPhone 6s family device—have specific fixes in 1.1.0.

Compatibility and jailbreak requirements

The current official listing says Drift supports iPhone, iPad and iPod touch on iOS 15.0 through 26.0 and requires a jailbreak. The package record names MobileSubstrate, PreferenceLoader and firmware 15.0 or newer as dependencies. That broad iOS range does not prove every device and jailbreak environment is compatible: the source does not provide a Dopamine, palera1n, RootHide, rootless or rootful support matrix.

Verify that your package manager can resolve the listed dependencies and that your current injection environment supports the package before buying. A package declaring iOS 26 compatibility does not make a jailbreak available for every iOS 26 device.

Drift’s core features

  • Up to 50 apps and folders in one horizontally scrollable dock.
  • Stock-like icon spacing and optional Snap to Icons alignment.
  • Edit-mode rearranging and removal.
  • Automatic return of excess dock items to the Home Screen if Drift is removed.
  • Optional edge haptics and effects, now expanded in version 1.1.0.

Before enabling Floating Dock

  1. Update Drift from its official Chariz package page and respring if your package manager requests it.
  2. Confirm the normal scrollable dock works before enabling Floating Dock, reflection or several effects at once.
  3. Enable Floating Dock by itself, then perform the required respring.
  4. Test the swipe-up gesture inside a non-critical app and confirm it does not conflict with another dock, multitasking or gesture tweak.
  5. If SpringBoard becomes unstable, enter your jailbreak’s safe mode, disable Drift or the conflicting Home Screen tweak, and respring before testing again.

Next Jailbreak has not performed device testing for this update. These steps separate the core dock from optional effects so a conflict is easier to identify without claiming compatibility the developer has not documented.

Troubleshooting

Floating Dock does not appear

Confirm the option is enabled and complete the respring named in the official changelog. If it still does not appear, temporarily disable other dock, multitasking and gesture tweaks before assuming the firmware is unsupported.

End icons remain faded while the dock is still

Check the installed Drift version in your package manager. Version 1.1.0 specifically fixes the fade overlay covering the icons at each end when the dock is not moving.

Haptic ticks do not work

Make sure haptics are enabled in Drift. The 1.1.0 changelog confirms a fix for iPhone 6s and 6s Plus, but does not promise haptic support on every device model.

SpringBoard enters safe mode

Disable newly enabled effects first, then test Drift without other dock or Home Screen modifications. If stability returns, re-enable features one at a time to identify the conflict. Do not repeatedly respring into a known crash loop.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current Drift version?

The official Chariz listing identifies Drift 1.1.0 as current and dates it November 5, 2026.

Does Drift 1.1.0 support iOS 26?

The official listing states iOS 15.0 through 26.0 on a jailbroken iPhone, iPad or iPod touch. It does not identify every supported jailbreak or device, so confirm the injection environment separately.

Does Floating Dock require a respring?

Yes. The 1.1.0 changelog explicitly says the optional Floating Dock feature needs a respring.

Can Drift hold folders?

Yes. The official feature list says its dock can hold up to 50 apps and folders.

What happens if Drift is removed?

The developer says dock items beyond the stock capacity return to the Home Screen. The listing does not promise that their exact order will be preserved.
