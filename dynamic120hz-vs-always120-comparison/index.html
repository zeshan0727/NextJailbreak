Dynamic120hz vs Always120: Which 120Hz Tweak Should You Use?

Both paid tweaks change how supported jailbroken iPhones handle high refresh rates, but their scope is different. Dynamic120hz is the newer, broader feature set with 120-FPS recording; Always120 is the lower-cost choice focused on adaptive and per-app locked modes.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 6, 2026 · Compared using Dynamic120hz 3.3.3-3 and Always120 1.2.0

The short answer

Dynamic120hz is the stronger current choice when your exact device and jailbreak are supported and you want the wider feature set. It is actively updated, lists newer ProMotion hardware, includes a game helper and added 120-FPS screen recording in version 3.3.3-3. Always120 is the value choice if its simpler adaptive and locked per-app modes meet your needs and your firmware falls inside its older compatibility window.

Neither tweak can make a physical 60Hz display refresh at 120Hz. Always120 explicitly says non-high-refresh devices can be adapted or locked at their existing 60Hz maximum. Package compatibility also does not prove that a jailbreak exists for every device on every listed iOS release.

Dynamic120hz: more features and newer maintenance

Dynamic120hz says it can dynamically remove an approximately 80Hz animation limit, choose injection behavior per app and expose a game helper for titles without their own 120Hz switch. The October 3 release adds 120-FPS screen recording and a better dynamic-unlock experience. The Havoc page lists iOS 15.0–17.3.1 and rootless support.

The developer’s description specifically names rootless jailbreaks such as Dopamine and XinaA15, plus Bootstrap or NathanLR environments, and lists iPhone 13, 14 and 15 Pro/Pro Max. Those environment names are not interchangeable: RootHide Bootstrap and NathanLR do not become full jailbreaks simply because a package can inject inside them.

Dynamic120hz also links buyers to a separate Hello CPU utility. Its thermal and CPU-frequency overrides are not required for the core refresh-rate feature. Thermal protections exist for safety; overriding them can increase heat, battery stress and instability, so this comparison does not recommend disabling them.

Always120: simpler adaptive and locked modes

Always120 offers a global adaptive mode that raises refresh rate while the view is moving and lowers it while stationary, plus a locked mode for selected apps. It also exposes a refresh-rate label. Version 1.2.0 added an option to disable the tweak below a user-selected battery level, giving it a straightforward battery-aware control that the Dynamic120hz listing does not advertise in the same form.

There is a documentation nuance worth noticing: Havoc’s structured compatibility field says iOS 14.0–16.5.1, while the developer description concentrates on iOS 15 and 16 and names tests on iPhone XR with iOS 15.1 and iPhone X with iOS 16.1.2 for 60Hz behavior. Treat the structured range as a package listing, not evidence that every feature has been tested on every point release.

Compatibility is more than the iOS number

Both listings require a jailbreak and mark rootless support. That does not mean an iPhone 15 Pro on any firmware in the package range is automatically jailbreakable. First verify that the device, chip and exact iOS build have a working jailbreak or supported injection environment; then verify that the package supports that environment.

For Dynamic120hz, the official description’s supported-hardware list stops at the iPhone 15 Pro family even though later iPhones exist. Do not infer iPhone 16, 17 or 18 support from the word “ProMotion.” For Always120, the page names iPhone 13/14 Pro generations and does not claim later models.

Battery, heat and Low Power Mode

Keeping more interfaces at a high refresh rate can increase display and graphics workload. Always120 says Low Power Mode can force the display down to 80Hz, while Dynamic120hz says Low Power Mode is not applicable. Always120’s battery-threshold option can automatically stop its effect below the chosen charge level.

Actual battery impact varies with screen brightness, foreground apps, temperature and how aggressively a mode is used. The official pages do not provide controlled battery benchmarks, so this article does not invent a percentage or claim that either tweak is more efficient.

Should you install both?

Do not assume the two tweaks are safe to combine. Both alter refresh-rate behavior and neither official listing recommends running them together. Use one at a time, restart or respring as instructed, and remove the first package before judging the second. If SpringBoard becomes unstable, disable tweak injection and uninstall the last change using our Dopamine recovery guide.

Which one should you buy?

  • Choose Dynamic120hz for its newer October 2026 release, broader control set, game helper and official 120-FPS recording feature—provided your exact ProMotion iPhone and jailbreak environment are supported.
  • Choose Always120 when you want the cheaper $1.99 option, primarily need adaptive or locked per-app behavior, and your device fits the developer’s older compatibility description.
  • Choose neither if your phone lacks a compatible jailbreak, your hardware is not documented, or you expect software to turn a 60Hz panel into a 120Hz panel.

Frequently asked questions

Which tweak is newer?

Dynamic120hz 3.3.3-3 was released October 3, 2026. Always120 1.2.0 was released August 22, 2025.

Can either tweak add 120Hz to a 60Hz iPhone?

No. Software cannot replace the physical display. Always120 says non-ProMotion models can be held at their existing 60Hz maximum.

Do both support rootless jailbreaks?

Yes, both current Havoc pages mark rootless support. The exact device, firmware, jailbreak and package environment must still match.

Which one supports 120-FPS screen recording?

Dynamic120hz 3.3.3-3 explicitly adds 120-FPS screen recording. Always120’s current changelog does not make that claim.
