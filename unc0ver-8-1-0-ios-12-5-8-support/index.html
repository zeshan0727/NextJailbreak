unc0ver 8.1.0 Adds iOS 12.5.6–12.5.8 Jailbreak Support

The unc0ver team has released version 8.1.0, extending the semi-untethered jailbreak to Apple’s final iOS 12 maintenance releases. The update matters most to owners of the iPhone 5s, iPhone 6 family and matching legacy iPads—but it does not add support for newer devices or firmware beyond unc0ver’s existing limits.

What unc0ver 8.1.0 changes

The official unc0ver release feed lists v8.1.0 with a publication time of October 1, 2026. Its changelog contains one focused addition: support for iOS 12.5.6, iOS 12.5.7 and iOS 12.5.8. The downloaded official IPA also identifies itself as version 8.1.0 and retains a minimum OS version of iOS 11.0.

This is a compatibility update for old firmware, not a new exploit for modern iPhones. It closes a gap left after unc0ver 7.0.2 supported iOS 12.5.5 while Apple later shipped three more iOS 12 releases. The project’s public download feed now places 8.1.0 ahead of 8.0.2.

Which devices receive the new support

Apple released iOS and iPadOS 12.5.8 on January 26, 2026 for the iPhone 5s, iPhone 6, iPhone 6 Plus, original iPad Air, iPad mini 2, iPad mini 3 and sixth-generation iPod touch. Those are the devices directly relevant to unc0ver 8.1.0’s new iOS 12.5.8 support.

  • iPhones: iPhone 5s, iPhone 6 and iPhone 6 Plus.
  • iPads: iPad Air, iPad mini 2 and iPad mini 3.
  • iPod: iPod touch (6th generation).

Compatibility follows the installed firmware as well as the hardware. An iPhone or iPad on iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, 26 or 27 does not become compatible merely because unc0ver has a new version number. Likewise, 8.1.0 does not extend the existing iOS 14.6–14.8 path beyond the A12 and A13 iPhone limits documented for that branch.

Why iOS 12.5.8 exists

Apple says iOS 12.5.8 extends a certificate needed for features including iMessage, FaceTime and device activation to continue working after January 2027. Apple’s security-release listing says the update has no published CVE entries. For owners who installed that maintenance release, unc0ver 8.1.0 restores a jailbreak route without requiring them to remain on 12.5.5.

That distinction matters because a certificate-maintenance update can still change the firmware build enough to break an older jailbreak’s version checks or exploit path. The new unc0ver build explicitly covers all three post-12.5.5 releases rather than asking users to treat them as interchangeable.

What did not change

The official 8.1.0 note does not claim new iOS 13 or iOS 14 reliability work, and it does not announce support for iOS 15 or later. The A12/A13 guidance and exploit-reliability fixes for iOS 14.6–14.8 remain the separate changes published with unc0ver 8.0.2 in 2021.

unc0ver also remains semi-untethered. After a full reboot, the device returns to a non-jailbroken state until the unc0ver app is run again. If the app is installed through a normal free Apple ID signing workflow, it generally needs to be re-signed every seven days; installation method and certificate status are separate from jailbreak compatibility.

Safe update checklist

  1. Open Settings → General → About and record the exact device model and iOS version.
  2. Back up important data before changing jailbreak software or restoring RootFS.
  3. Download 8.1.0 only from the official unc0ver site; the official feed points to the project’s own 8.1.0 IPA.
  4. Remove or replace an expired older unc0ver app while preserving the device’s data backup.
  5. Confirm that the installed app reports version 8.1.0 before using it on iOS 12.5.6–12.5.8.

Do not use third-party pages that claim unc0ver works on iOS 15, 16, 17, 18, 26 or 27. The official project site’s broad headline stops at iOS 14.8, and even that upper range is subject to device-specific restrictions.

How this compares with other legacy-device routes

unc0ver’s advantage on these iOS 12 builds is an on-device semi-untethered workflow after the app is signed. Checkra1n-style tools instead rely on the checkm8 bootrom path and normally need a computer after each reboot. Which route is preferable depends on the exact device, firmware, computer access and the package environment already installed.

Readers working on newer firmware should not extrapolate from this release. Use the iOS 16 jailbreak guide for current device-specific options, or compare Dopamine and palera1n where those tools are actually applicable.

Bottom line

unc0ver 8.1.0 is a narrow but valuable maintenance release. It gives the last iOS 12 devices an official unc0ver build for 12.5.6, 12.5.7 and 12.5.8, matching Apple’s final certificate-maintenance firmware. It is not evidence of an unc0ver comeback on current iOS versions, and it does not erase the project’s existing device limits on iOS 14.6–14.8.
