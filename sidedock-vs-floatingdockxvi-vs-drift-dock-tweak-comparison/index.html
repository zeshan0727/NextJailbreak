SideDock 1.0.1 vs FloatingDockXVI 2.3 vs Drift 1.1.0: Which Dock Tweak Fits?

These three tweaks solve different problems. SideDock builds a vertical macOS-style launcher for rootless iOS 17–18. FloatingDockXVI is a free iPad-style dock for iOS 15–16. Drift is a $0.99 scrollable dock with effects and an optional floating mode across a much broader declared iOS range—but without a published jailbreak-by-jailbreak matrix.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published November 28, 2026 · Official sources checked November 28, 2026

Compatibility is not jailbreak availability. A package’s iOS range does not mean every device on those versions can be jailbroken. Confirm the exact device, firmware, architecture, bootstrap and tweak-injection environment first.

Quick verdict

  • Choose SideDock 1.0.1 if a vertical right-edge dock, icon magnification and an in-app app switcher are the goal on a supported rootless iOS 17–18 setup.
  • Choose FloatingDockXVI 2.3 for the closest free, focused iPad-style Dock on officially listed iOS 15–16, with recents, App Library access and rootless support.
  • Choose Drift 1.1.0 for a horizontally scrollable dock holding up to 50 apps or folders, visual effects and optional floating access inside apps—only after checking its dependencies and environment with your package manager.
  • Do not stack them. All three alter SpringBoard’s dock. The official sources do not promise that any pair can safely control it at the same time.

What each tweak actually changes

SideDock: a vertical launcher, not an iPad Dock clone

SideDock moves the Dock to the right edge and uses a vertical layout with macOS-style magnification. It can show between one and five recent apps, auto-hide, accept drag and drop, appear inside apps as a switcher and expand the Home Screen grid to as many as six columns by eight rows. Version 1.0.1’s important change is practical: icons and widgets now leave space for that dock instead of being drawn underneath it.

This is the most visually distinct option and the most narrowly documented environment. Havoc lists rootless support and the developer names Dopamine and palera1n, with tests reported on an iPhone 15 Pro Max running iOS 17.3.1 and on iOS 18.6.2. Those examples are not a universal device matrix, and the listing does not explicitly promise RootHide support.

FloatingDockXVI: the focused iPad-style option

FloatingDockXVI keeps the Dock at the bottom and adds the parts people usually mean by “iPad-style”: a floating background, recent applications and an App Library shortcut. Its settings let you select Recents, Library, both or neither, hide the background or separator, and set maximum recent and pinned-app counts. The code is published by ETHN on GitHub.

Havoc still identifies 2.3 as current. That April 26, 2024 release fixes a recents crash; older history also records iOS 16.5 crash handling, rootless support in 1.2 and Dopamine support in 1.3. The age of the current version is not proof that it is obsolete, but its official ceiling remains iOS 16.7.4. Do not extrapolate that to iOS 17, 18 or 26.

Drift: capacity and effects first, floating mode second

Drift’s main design is a horizontal, scrollable Dock that can hold up to 50 apps and folders. Version 1.1.0 adds five edge effects, four scrolling effects, optional icon reflection and a position indicator. It also offers an iPad-style Floating Dock on iPhone that can be revealed inside an app with a swipe up.

That makes Drift the better fit when capacity and motion effects matter more than reproducing the stock iPad Dock. Chariz lists iPhone, iPad and iPod touch on iOS 15.0–26.0 and names MobileSubstrate and PreferenceLoader dependencies, but it does not publish a Dopamine, palera1n, RootHide, rootless or rootful matrix. Treat the broad firmware declaration as package metadata, not proof that every current injection environment works.

Official source visuals: three different designs

Which tweak fits each iOS version?

On iOS 15 or 16: FloatingDockXVI is the clearest free choice when the desired result is an iPad-like Dock. Drift is the alternative when extra capacity and effects are more important. SideDock does not list these versions.

On iOS 17 or 18: SideDock has the strongest environment-specific documentation for conventional rootless setups and is the only option here built around a vertical dock. Drift declares those firmware versions too, but its source does not state which jailbreak environments were tested. FloatingDockXVI stops at iOS 16.7.4.

On iOS 19 through 26: only Drift’s package listing declares this range. That does not create a jailbreak for those versions or validate every device. Install only if a legitimate jailbreak and package manager can satisfy the package’s dependencies for the exact setup.

Safe installation and conflict plan

  1. Record the exact device, iOS build, jailbreak, bootstrap and injection provider. Check the package architecture in the package-manager transaction.
  2. Back up the current Home Screen arrangement where the jailbreak or layout tools permit it. Screenshots are also useful for manual restoration.
  3. Remove or fully disable any other dock, icon-layout, App Library or gesture tweak that controls the same area. A preference toggle may not unload every hook until after a respring.
  4. Install only from the official Havoc or Chariz source. Confirm the exact identifiers and versions shown in the package manager.
  5. Respring, test the default configuration first, and enable one feature group at a time. Test rotation, folders, App Library, App Switcher and opening the dock from inside apps.
  6. If SpringBoard becomes unstable, reboot or start the jailbreak without tweak injection, remove the new dock tweak, then re-enable injection. Do not repeatedly respring into a known crash loop.

Settings page is blank or missing

Refresh sources and verify PreferenceLoader before reinstalling the tweak. SideDock specifically warns that iOS 18 needs a compatible PreferenceLoader build. Drift also declares PreferenceLoader as a dependency. A package can be active while its preference bundle fails to load.

Recent apps crash or behave strangely

For FloatingDockXVI, first verify version 2.3 because that release specifically fixes a recents crash. Reset custom recent-app counts to defaults and test without another App Switcher or dock tweak. For SideDock, reduce the recent-app count before adding other layout changes.

Icons overlap or the old Dock remains

Confirm SideDock is 1.0.1, which addresses grid overlap, then respring after changing its grid. With any of the three, remove the previously installed dock tweak rather than leaving two packages to modify the same SpringBoard classes.

Final recommendation

SideDock 1.0.1 is the right specialist for a vertical, magnifying launcher on its documented rootless iOS 17–18 range. FloatingDockXVI 2.3 is the best no-cost iPad-style choice for iOS 15–16 and has inspectable source code. Drift 1.1.0 wins for capacity and effects, with floating access as an optional extra, but its broad iOS declaration requires the most careful environment verification.

For deeper checks, read the existing SideDock 1.0.1 update and Drift 1.1.0 update. Before buying or installing, use the tweak compatibility checklist, the rootless tweak collection and the bad-tweak recovery guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which dock tweak is best for iOS 16?

FloatingDockXVI 2.3 is the focused free choice officially listed for iOS 15.0–16.7.4. Drift 1.1.0 also lists iOS 16, but it targets a scrollable, effects-heavy dock and lacks a published jailbreak-environment matrix.

Which one supports RootHide?

None of the three official listings explicitly promises RootHide support. “Rootless” does not automatically mean native RootHide compatibility.

Can SideDock, FloatingDockXVI and Drift be installed together?

No official source promises safe stacking. Use one dock engine at a time because all three modify SpringBoard’s Dock behavior.

Is FloatingDockXVI outdated because version 2.3 is from 2024?

It remains the current version on Havoc, but its official support range still ends at iOS 16.7.4. Do not infer support for newer firmware from package availability.
