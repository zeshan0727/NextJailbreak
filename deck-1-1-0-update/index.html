Deck 1.2.0 Adds Side-by-Side Control Center and Safer Close All Controls

Deck 1.2.0 expands the card-based app switcher with drag-to-pin gestures, optional protection for the app playing audio, a quick-action strip and a Side by Side Control Center page. Chariz continues to list iPhone, iPad and iPod touch support on iOS 15.0–18.7, but the native Control Center module view is specifically an iOS 18 feature.

What changed in Deck 1.2.0

Chariz’s November 6 release feed lists five additions for Deck 1.2.0. A downward drag on an app card now pins or unpins that app. An optional Pin Playing App setting protects the app currently playing audio when Close All runs. The update also adds quick actions for respring, unpin all, close all and next track, with the device’s battery level shown alongside them.

The largest layout change is Side by Side. Instead of keeping Deck’s controls beneath the app cards, this mode places Control Center on a neighboring page that is one horizontal swipe away. On iOS 18 specifically, the page can display Control Center’s own modules; long-pressing one opens its expanded control, matching the interaction in Apple’s Control Center. The changelog does not say that native modules are available on iOS 15–17, so users on those releases should not treat the iOS 18 behavior as universal.

  • Drag a card down to pin or unpin it.
  • Optionally protect the currently playing audio app from Close All.
  • Use quick actions for respring, unpin all, close all and next track.
  • See the battery level in the quick-action area.
  • Swipe to a separate Side by Side Control Center page.
  • On iOS 18, use native Control Center modules and long-press their expanded views.

Why the pinning changes are useful

Deck already let pinned apps survive Close All, but 1.2.0 reduces the steps required to manage that list. Drag-to-pin is useful when the app you want to protect is already visible in the switcher; Unpin All provides a clean reset when the list has grown stale.

Pin Playing App solves a different problem. If you routinely clear the switcher while music, a podcast or another audio session is active, Deck can leave that playback app open without requiring a permanent manual pin. Because the option is described in terms of the app currently playing audio, it should not be assumed to preserve paused apps indefinitely or protect every app with background activity.

Side by Side versus the original control layouts

The original Deck release offered Control Center cards beneath the app grid or carousel, plus a compact bar for brightness, volume, music and Control Center. Version 1.2.0 does not remove those documented layouts; it adds a separate page for users who would rather keep the app cards and controls spatially distinct.

Choose the under-card layout if you want apps and common toggles visible in one view. Choose the compact bar when vertical space matters. Side by Side is the clearer option when you want a fuller control page without reducing the app-card area, and it is most capable on iOS 18 because that is where the official changelog promises Apple’s own modules and their long-press views.

Compatibility and jailbreak-environment limits

The current Chariz listing says Deck supports iPhone, iPad and iPod touch running iOS 15.0 through 18.7 and requires a jailbreak. The package index publishes version 1.2.0 in both iphoneos-arm and iphoneos-arm64 stanzas, each depending on MobileSubstrate, PreferenceLoader and firmware 15.0 or newer.

Those package architectures are useful installation metadata, but they do not prove compatibility with every jailbreak, device model or tweak-injection environment. Chariz does not explicitly name Dopamine, palera1n, RootHide or a supported-device matrix on the listing. Before buying, compare your setup with the site’s tweak compatibility checklist and verify that your package manager resolves the correct architecture and dependencies. Deck remains listed at $2.99 before any applicable tax.

Safe update and troubleshooting checklist

  1. Refresh sources in your package manager and confirm that the candidate version is 1.2.0 from Chariz, package identifier com.antonym.deck.
  2. Note your current Deck layout and pin choices before updating. This gives you a known configuration to restore if the new controls feel wrong.
  3. After updating, test manual pinning with one disposable app before relying on Close All. Then start audio and test Pin Playing App separately.
  4. If Side by Side appears but native modules do not, check the iOS version first. The official changelog limits the native-module and long-press behavior to iOS 18.
  5. If Deck does not load, temporarily disable other app-switcher or Control Center tweaks, then retest. Two tweaks modifying the same SpringBoard surfaces can conflict even when each supports the installed iOS version.
  6. If SpringBoard becomes unusable, boot or re-jailbreak with tweak injection disabled and remove or disable Deck. The bad-tweak recovery guide explains that recovery pattern for Dopamine users.

Do not download a repackaged copy to work around a dependency or license error. Refresh Chariz, resolve the reported dependency through your package manager and use the official purchase flow. Readers comparing package managers can also use the Sileo, Zebra and Cydia comparison.

What Deck 1.2.0 does not claim

The release notes do not claim iOS 19–27 support, RootHide support, compatibility with every other switcher or Control Center tweak, performance gains, or lower memory use. They also do not expand the listed device family beyond iPhone, iPad and iPod touch. Keeping those limits separate from the feature list prevents a broad package compatibility range from being mistaken for universal jailbreak availability.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current Deck version?

Chariz and its package index list Deck 1.2.0. The update was posted in the official release feed on November 6, 2026.

Which iOS versions does Deck support?

The official listing states iOS 15.0 through 18.7 on jailbroken iPhone, iPad and iPod touch devices. Native Control Center modules in Side by Side are specifically documented for iOS 18.

Does Deck 1.2.0 support rootless jailbreaks?

The repository publishes both iphoneos-arm and iphoneos-arm64 package stanzas, but the listing does not explicitly name rootful, rootless, RootHide or individual jailbreak tools. Confirm the package architecture and dependencies in your own package manager before purchase.

How does Pin Playing App differ from a normal pin?

A normal pin is assigned to a selected card. Pin Playing App is an optional rule that protects the app currently playing audio when Close All is used.

Do settings require a respring?

The product description says configuration changes apply instantly, but 1.2.0 also adds a respring quick action. Follow any package-manager prompt after installation or update.
