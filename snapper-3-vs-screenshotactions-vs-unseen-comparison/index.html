Snapper 3 vs ScreenshotActions vs Unseen: Which Screenshot Tweak Fits You?

All three packages affect screenshots, but they solve different problems. Choose by the action you need first—capture and pin, act on the stock preview, or reduce app-side detection—not by the shared “Screenshots” tag.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 21, 2026 · Versions and compatibility checked against official Havoc pages and Unseen’s developer repository

Quick verdict

  • Choose Snapper 3 if you want the broadest capture workflow: crop, pin over other apps, copy, share, save, run OCR, scan QR codes, and revisit capture history.
  • Choose ScreenshotActions if you use supported older firmware and only want faster actions from Apple’s stock screenshot preview.
  • Choose Unseen if your problem is an ordinary app detecting a screenshot or recording, hiding content, or reacting to capture state. It is not an editor or pinning utility.
They are not three versions of the same idea. Snapper 3 and ScreenshotActions change the capture workflow; Unseen changes how ordinary apps perceive or interfere with capture. Do not install multiple system-wide hooks merely because their features sound adjacent—check developer support and test one change at a time.

Snapper 3: the complete capture toolbox

Snapper 3 is the clear choice when a screenshot should remain useful after you take it. Its official listing centers on cropped captures that can stay pinned above other apps. It also lists instant copy, share and save actions, OCR, on-screen QR scanning, capture history, Imgur upload, and plugin support.

That makes it the best fit for copying text from a non-selectable interface, keeping reference details visible while working in another app, or extracting only the region you need. Its 1.2.1 update improved support for alternative jailbreaks, added an option to remove the source app’s bundle identifier from snaps, and improved localization.

The tradeoffs are price and scope. It is the only paid package in this comparison, and the official marketplace ceiling is iOS 17.2. Do not interpret “Supports Rootless” as automatic support for iOS 18 or newer.

ScreenshotActions: fastest route from preview to action

ScreenshotActions keeps Apple’s normal screenshot experience and adds a shortcut layer. The developer’s documented gesture is to long-press the screenshot preview in the lower-left corner, then choose an action from the alert. The official screenshot shows choices such as Copy and Delete, Save, Share, Upload, and Save to Album.

That narrow design is its strength: there is less to learn if your only complaint is the number of taps required after taking a normal screenshot. It does not advertise Snapper 3’s floating pinned captures, OCR, QR scanning, or history.

The major limitation is firmware. Havoc lists version 1.2 for iOS 12.0 through 15.7.5. A package can advertise rootless support and still have a lower firmware ceiling; users on iOS 16 or later should not infer compatibility from the rootless badge alone.

Unseen: capture privacy and app interference

Unseen is the outlier—and that is exactly why it belongs in this comparison. It does not make screenshots easier to crop or manage. Its developer says it reduces ordinary apps’ ability to detect or interfere with screenshots, screen recordings, and hidden-layer flags.

The official source documents switches for revealing ordinary app content hidden through layer flags, hiding screenshot events, hiding recording or mirroring state, and—on iOS 17 or later—protecting system interface elements that should remain hidden. The project explicitly says Reveal Hidden Content does not bypass DRM or system copyright protections.

Havoc lists version 0.0.5 for iOS 15.0 through 18.7.9 with rootless support. The developer repository discusses rootful, rootless, and RootHide application layouts and testing across several iOS generations. That source evidence is more specific than treating the package’s minimum firmware dependency as proof of every possible setup.

Which one should you install?

You frequently reference information across apps

Pick Snapper 3. Pinning is the differentiator, while OCR and QR recognition reduce the need for separate utilities.

You want a shorter post-screenshot workflow on iOS 15 or older

Pick ScreenshotActions if your exact firmware is inside its iOS 12.0–15.7.5 listing. It is the focused option for acting on the stock preview.

An app reacts to screenshots or recordings

Review Unseen. Its controls target event detection, recording state, and ordinary hidden layers. Respect privacy, copyright, workplace policy, and applicable law; the developer does not present it as a DRM bypass.

You need annotation, scrolling capture, OCR, translation and pinning together

None of these three exactly matches that combined brief. Read our Prism vs Circle to Search comparison, where Prism is the capture-and-annotation option.

Installation cautions

  • Use the official package pages linked below; do not install repackaged paid tweaks.
  • Confirm the package’s stated iOS range and your actual jailbreak environment separately.
  • Install one system-wide screenshot hook at a time, respring, and test the stock screenshot gesture before adding another.
  • If SpringBoard or apps crash, disable the newest package first and collect a crash log.
  • Unseen’s capture controls do not remove your responsibility to protect other people’s privacy and copyrighted material.
