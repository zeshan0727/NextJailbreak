Dynamic120hz 3.3.3-3 Adds 120 FPS Screen Recording

The paid rootless tweak now supports recording video at 120 FPS and promises a better dynamic 120Hz unlock experience on its listed ProMotion iPhones.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 5, 2026 · Version 3.3.3-3 released October 3, 2026

What changed in 3.3.3-3?

The official two-line changelog says Dynamic120hz 3.3.3-3 delivers a “better dynamic 120Hz unlock experience” and adds 120-FPS screen-recording support. The latter is the concrete new capability: users who rely on the tweak’s higher-refresh-rate path can now capture compatible screen recordings above the usual 60-FPS ceiling.

The developer does not document a longer technical change list, name specific apps for the recording feature or promise that every app and game will render at 120 FPS. Treat the update as support for the capture path—not proof that all recorded content will contain 120 distinct frames every second.

What Dynamic120hz does

Dynamic120hz targets animation paths that the developer says are commonly capped around 80Hz, then lets users request a dynamic 120Hz mode. It also provides per-app injection controls and a game helper for titles that do not expose their own 120Hz option. A debug HUD can help show system-level information, but perceived smoothness, frame delivery and recording output still depend on the app, device load and display pipeline.

Compatibility is narrower than the iOS range

Havoc lists an iOS package range of 15.0–17.3.1, jailbreak required, with rootless support. The package description is more specific about hardware: it names the iPhone 13 Pro and Pro Max, 14 Pro and Pro Max, and 15 Pro and Pro Max. Those are the developer’s listed ProMotion targets; the page does not currently claim iPhone 16-series support.

The developer names rootless jailbreak environments such as Dopamine and XinaA15, and also describes Bootstrap or NathanLR semi-jailbreak support. A package’s firmware declaration does not create a jailbreak for every device on that range. Confirm that your exact iPhone and firmware have a compatible environment before buying or installing. Our Dopamine, NathanLR and RootHide comparison explains why these environments are not interchangeable.

Battery and thermal trade-offs matter

Higher refresh rates and 120-FPS capture can increase graphics, storage and battery demand. Havoc explicitly says Low Power Mode is not applicable. Dynamic120hz also links buyers to the developer’s separate Hello CPU utility, which can override thermal-related brightness or performance reductions and alter CPU-frequency behavior.

Thermal controls are safety mechanisms. Disabling them can increase heat, battery stress and instability, so the separate utility should not be treated as a requirement for the core tweak or as a harmless performance switch. Change one setting at a time, watch device temperature and restore defaults if the phone becomes unusually hot or unstable.

Before you update

Version 3.3.3-3 is a 381.6 KB paid package on Havoc. Repository metadata identifies package com.zqbb.dynamic120hz, rootless architecture and dependencies on a hooking platform, PreferenceLoader and AltList. It also conflicts with older Fix120hz package identifiers, so remove conflicting refresh-rate tweaks rather than injecting two implementations at once.

Use the official Havoc listing for the current package and version history. If you are troubleshooting a conflict, follow our jailbreak tweak compatibility checklist, test without other display or gaming tweaks, and keep a known-good configuration you can restore.
