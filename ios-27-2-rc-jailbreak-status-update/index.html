iOS 27.2 Released With Security Fixes—Still No Public Jailbreak

Apple released iOS and iPadOS 27.2 on October 26, 2026. The security notes document numerous memory-safety fixes and credit several Calif researchers, but neither Apple nor Calif published a jailbreak, exploit chain or iOS 27.2 support matrix.

By Zeeshan BarviPublished October 19, 2026 · Updated October 26, 20268 min read
Verified status: iOS 27.2 is public. Apple lists two iOS builds—24B91020 and 24B119—and iPadOS 27.2 build 24B119. No public iOS 27.2 jailbreak or consumer installation tool was announced with them.

What Apple released on October 26

Apple’s developer releases page lists iOS 27.2 twice, as builds 24B91020 and 24B119, plus iPadOS 27.2 build 24B119. The public listing does not say which iPhone models receive each iOS build, so this article does not guess at that mapping.

Apple’s security page says iOS and iPadOS 27.2 are available for iPhone 11 and later, iPad Pro 12.9-inch fourth generation and later, iPad Pro 11-inch second generation and later, iPad Air fourth generation and later, iPad ninth generation and later, and iPad mini sixth generation and later.

The same release day also brought iOS and iPadOS 26.7.2 for iPhone 11 and later plus a slightly broader set of supported iPads, and iOS and iPadOS 18.7.11 for the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR plus older supported iPads. Those are security updates, not jailbreak releases.

The security fixes matter, but they are not a jailbreak

Apple’s iOS 27.2 security document is extensive. It includes Apple Neural Engine issues that could allow an app to corrupt or write kernel memory, AVEVideoEncoder and CoreAudio memory-safety bugs, several kernel use-after-free and out-of-bounds-write fixes, and a Compression issue that could disclose kernel memory.

Those descriptions are relevant to security research, but a CVE with “kernel memory” in its impact is not automatically a usable jailbreak. A modern jailbreak normally needs a workable entry point, privilege escalation, mitigation bypasses, code-signing work, a bootstrap and exact hardware and firmware support. Apple’s document records fixed security issues; it does not provide that chain or say the bugs were combined.

What the Calif credits do—and do not—show

Apple credits Calif researchers on several iOS 27.2 entries, including App Intents CVE-2026-112692, AVEVideoEncoder CVE-2026-112705, CoreAudio and CoreGraphics reports, and a WebKit issue. That confirms Calif researchers reported real vulnerabilities fixed in this release.

It does not prove that Apple disclosed or fully patched Calif’s private Chollima jailbreak chain. Apple’s notes never name Chollima, never connect the credited CVEs into a jailbreak chain and never say that the private iPhone Duo demonstration used those exact bugs. Treating a researcher credit as a public exploit release would overstate the evidence.

The separate iOS 27 jailbreak status report remains the source for Calif’s demonstrated devices and public limits. The demonstration did not include a consumer download, compatibility list or release date.

iOS 18.7.11 fixes an exploited issue

Apple’s shorter iOS 18.7.11 notes deserve attention. The company says a CoreGraphics issue could lead to arbitrary code execution when processing a maliciously crafted file, and that it is aware of a report the issue may have been used in an extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals on iOS versions before iOS 27.

That warning is a reason for affected users to weigh security heavily. Apple does not call the issue a public jailbreak, publish exploit code or claim general device compromise. A targeted-attack report and a consumer jailbreak are different things.

Should jailbreak-focused users update?

For ordinary users—especially anyone at higher risk of targeted attacks—the current security release is the safer default. Apple’s updates address exploitable memory-safety, privacy and sandbox problems. Staying on older firmware preserves exposure to bugs that Apple has now documented as fixed.

For researchers or users deliberately preserving a firmware version, the tradeoff is different: iOS 27.2 has no announced jailbreak advantage, and a private demonstration on an earlier build does not establish support. Apple’s security-release page also warns that after an iOS software update is installed, it cannot be downgraded to the previous version. Make that decision before updating, keep a current encrypted backup and do not rely on a rumor or an old signing screenshot.

  • Do not download “iOS 27.2 jailbreak” profiles or IPAs: no verified developer released one with Apple’s update.
  • Do not infer compatibility from a tweak listing: a package can name iOS 27 while no public jailbreak exists for your device and build.
  • Do not equate a CVE with a finished tool: the security notes describe individual fixed bugs, not a maintained bootstrap.
  • Prioritize security on a primary device: Apple’s notes include memory-corruption and privacy fixes, while 18.7.11 addresses an issue reported as exploited in targeted attacks.
  • Wait for primary-source proof: a real jailbreak release should provide an official download, exact device and firmware checks, and installation guidance.

Current public-jailbreak boundary

No official Dopamine, palera1n, RootHide or TrollStore release page cited by Next Jailbreak announces general iOS 27.2 support. palera1n remains hardware-limited to checkm8-era devices, while current iOS 27.2 availability starts with iPhone 11 on Apple’s security page. TrollStore’s official installation range also does not extend to iOS 27.2.

That boundary can change only when a primary developer publishes new evidence or tooling. Until then, the accurate status is simple: iOS 27.2 is a security release with interesting vulnerability disclosures, but it is not a public jailbreak release.

Frequently asked questions

Is iOS 27.2 jailbroken?

No public jailbreak, installation tool or iOS 27.2 support matrix accompanied Apple’s release.

Why are there two iOS 27.2 build numbers?

Apple lists builds 24B91020 and 24B119. Its public developer page does not map those entries to individual iPhone models, so users should check the build offered on their exact device.

Did Apple patch Calif’s private jailbreak?

Apple credits Calif researchers on several fixes, but the notes do not identify Chollima or say the credited CVEs form the private jailbreak chain.

Do kernel-memory CVEs mean a jailbreak is coming?

No. They can be important research primitives, but a public jailbreak requires a complete, reliable chain plus device support, code-signing work, bootstrap integration and a maintained release.

Can I downgrade after installing iOS 27.2?

Apple’s security-release page warns that after an iOS update is installed, it cannot be downgraded to the previous version.

Does iOS 18.7.11 contain an actively exploited fix?

Apple says a CoreGraphics issue may have been exploited in an extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals on versions before iOS 27. That is not the same as a public jailbreak.
