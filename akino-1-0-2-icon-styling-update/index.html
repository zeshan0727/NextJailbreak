Akino 1.0.2 Fixes iOS 17 Floating Dock Crash and Expands Icon Styling

Akino applies dark, clear and tinted treatments to existing iPhone app icons on older iOS versions. Version 1.0.2 addresses a floating-dock crash on iOS 17, themes more previously missed apps, and improves the visibility of Settings icons in light appearance.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 2, 2026 · Update released October 1, 2026

What changed in Akino 1.0.2

Foxfort Mobile released Akino 1.0.2 on October 1, 2026. The official changelog lists three changes: a fix for a crash involving the floating dock on iOS 17, support for many app icons that were not being themed, and a correction for Settings icons that were difficult to see in light appearance. Version 1.0.1, published September 29, was the initial release, making 1.0.2 the current build.

The dock fix is the clearest reason for existing users to update. The wider icon coverage is useful too, but the developer still warns that some complex or game icons may render poorly and provides a reporting option in Akino’s settings.

How Akino styles icons

Akino brings an iOS 26-style icon-customization workflow to older jailbroken systems without claiming to reproduce Apple’s exact iOS 26 artwork. It dynamically processes existing Apple and third-party icons instead of requiring a hand-made replacement theme for every app.

The four documented effects are Default, Dark, Clear and Tinted. Clear can be fully transparent or frosted, while appearance can follow the system or be forced to light or dark. The controls live in the normal Home Screen editing flow: enter edit mode, open Customise, and choose a style.

Where the treatment appears

The developer says Akino styles icons beyond the Home Screen, including Settings, Spotlight, the Share Sheet and other interfaces that display app icons. Newly installed apps should be styled automatically. Experimental widget tinting is included, though the package page cautions that it may not work perfectly with every third-party widget.

Because several system surfaces are involved, update conservatively: update Akino by itself, respring, inspect the Home Screen and Settings first, then check Spotlight, Share Sheet and the widgets you use.

Compatibility has two hard exclusions

Havoc lists Akino 1.0.2 for iOS 15.0 through iOS 17.7.11. The US$3.99 package is marked rootless. Crucially, the developer explicitly says it is not compatible with RootHide or Relaxin. “Rootless” must not be read as support for every modern jailbreak environment.

Akino also conflicts with other icon-theming engines. The official page names SnowBoard and Miikky and recommends uninstalling them while using Akino. Follow that guidance rather than forcing two theming engines to coexist.

The Havoc range describes package compatibility, not jailbreak availability. It does not mean every device on every listed iOS release can be jailbroken. Confirm the device, chip and jailbreak separately before buying. See our compatibility checklist and rootless tweaks hub for the distinction.

Should existing users update?

For iOS 17 users with a floating dock, 1.0.2 directly targets a reported crash. Users seeing unthemed icons or weak Settings contrast also have documented fixes to evaluate. If 1.0.1 is stable and none applies, the changelog does not describe unrelated new features that require an urgent change.

Before updating, remove or avoid the named theming conflicts and verify the environment is neither RootHide nor Relaxin. After respringing, test a stock and third-party icon, switch between light and dark appearance, then inspect Settings. Report remaining complex-icon failures through Akino rather than treating the broad iOS range as a guarantee for every asset.
