PowerTrace 1.0.2b Fixes Rootless Saving and Adds Lock Screen Controls

PowerTrace 1.0.2b fixes a rootless permissions problem that could stop the tweak from saving data, playing alert sounds, refreshing fallback battery information, or maintaining crash protection. The update also lets users hide overlapping Lock Screen elements and resume the tweak after a protective pause.

By Zeeshan BarviPublished October 18, 20266 min read
Release snapshot: Havoc lists PowerTrace 1.0.2b at $1.99, released October 17, 2026, for rootless jailbreaks on iOS 15.0–18.5. Those package limits do not mean every device in that range currently has a compatible jailbreak.

What changed in PowerTrace 1.0.2b?

The most important fix is not cosmetic. According to the official changelog, PowerTrace could fail to save its data because of incorrect data-folder permissions on rootless jailbreaks. That failure affected alert sounds, the battery-information fallback and the tweak’s own crash-protection state. Version 1.0.2b corrects the permissions issue.

The update also adds two instant Lock Screen controls. Users can hide Apple’s “Swipe up to unlock” text when it overlaps the HUD and optionally hide the Home Bar pill. Neither action requires a respring.

  • Rootless data fix: restores saving for alerts, fallback readings and crash protection.
  • Hide “Swipe up to unlock”: removes text that can overlap the battery HUD.
  • Hide Home Bar: removes the bottom Lock Screen pill when desired.
  • Resume button: explains a protective pause and lets the user restart PowerTrace from Settings.
  • Smarter crash protection: quick resprings during several tweak installs should no longer make PowerTrace disable itself unnecessarily.

What the Lock Screen HUD measures

PowerTrace places a compact status panel between the flashlight and camera buttons. Its official description lists battery percentage, temperature in Celsius or Fahrenheit, charging or discharging current in milliamps, charging power in watts, charging status and an estimated time to full charge.

The HUD can also show estimated screen-on and screen-off drain rates, time spent in those states, estimated standby or usage time, battery health and cycle count. Settings expose further hardware details such as full-charge capacity, design capacity, Qmax, voltage, manufacturer and serial number.

Settings and alerts

The tweak lets users select which fields appear, change text color and size, move the HUD vertically and adjust its refresh interval. Optional haptic and sound alerts can fire when the battery reaches full charge or crosses a configurable high-temperature threshold.

PowerTrace can copy a diagnostic report for reference. Because the detailed-information screen may include a battery serial number and hardware identifiers, review and redact that report before posting it publicly or attaching it to a support request.

Compatibility and requirements

The official Havoc metadata lists package identifier com.t4mag0.powertrace, version 1.0.2b, arm64e architecture, rootless support and an iOS range of 15.0 through 18.5. A jailbreak is required. The listing does not claim rootful or RootHide support, so do not convert or install the package into another environment unless the developer publishes compatible instructions.

Package compatibility is separate from jailbreak availability. For example, being on iOS 18.5 does not by itself establish that a particular device can run a supported rootless jailbreak. Confirm the exact device, firmware and active jailbreak before purchasing.

How to update safely

  1. Open Havoc in Sileo or Zebra and refresh sources.
  2. Confirm the package identifier is com.t4mag0.powertrace and the version is 1.0.2b.
  3. Check that the current jailbreak is rootless and the device is within the developer’s iOS 15.0–18.5 range.
  4. Review the transaction queue for unexpected removals or architecture conversions.
  5. Install the update and perform the restart action requested by the package manager.
  6. Open PowerTrace settings and confirm the HUD fields, alert threshold and refresh interval before relying on its readings.

If PowerTrace reports that it paused itself, version 1.0.2b now explains the reason and provides a Resume PowerTrace button. If SpringBoard continues to crash, leave the tweak disabled and collect a complete report using the KrashKop crash-log guide rather than repeatedly forcing it to run.

PowerTrace versus broader battery tools

PowerTrace is primarily a Lock Screen HUD with detailed battery readings in Settings. Users who want longer-form graphs, plug-in analysis or different system views can also review the existing Battman 1.1.0 coverage. Their feature focus differs, so choose based on where and how you want to inspect battery data rather than assuming one set of readings replaces the other.

Frequently asked questions

What is the latest PowerTrace version?

Havoc lists version 1.0.2b, published October 17, 2026.

Does PowerTrace support RootHide?

The official listing explicitly says rootless. It does not claim RootHide support, so use only the environment documented by the developer.

Does it work on iOS 19 or iOS 26?

No such support is listed. The official compatibility range is iOS 15.0–18.5.

Why did PowerTrace pause itself?

Its crash protection can pause the tweak after instability. Version 1.0.2b improves the logic so ordinary quick resprings are less likely to trigger it and adds a one-tap resume control with an explanation.

Is the battery data an Apple service diagnosis?

No. Treat it as monitoring information and trends. Use Apple or an authorized service provider for hardware-service decisions.
