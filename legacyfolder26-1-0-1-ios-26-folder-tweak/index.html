PrimalFolderPro vs LegacyFolder26: Which Folder Tweak Should You Use?

Choose PrimalFolderPro for larger grids, alternate folder icons and Exsto quick launch. Choose LegacyFolder26 for a focused iOS 6-style folder panel at a lower price.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 12, 2026 · Updated October 31, 2026 from both current Havoc listings and their embedded official media

Package compatibility is not jailbreak availability. Havoc lists PrimalFolderPro for iOS 14.0–26.0.1 and LegacyFolder26 for iOS 15.0–26.0.1, but your exact device and firmware still need a compatible jailbreak and tweak injection.

Quick answer

  • Pick PrimalFolderPro for 4×5 iPhone folders, 5×6 iPad folders, five folder-icon styles, title and color controls, or the Exsto radial launcher.
  • Pick LegacyFolder26 for the wide iOS 6-style opening panel, blur or glass appearances, or the lower $1.99 price.
  • Pick neither yet if you cannot confirm a compatible jailbreak, cannot recover from SpringBoard Safe Mode, or need an officially documented pairing with another folder tweak.

What PrimalFolderPro does better

PrimalFolderPro is the broader productivity option. It increases folder capacity, can change a folder icon to the first app, another app, a 2×2 preview or no icon, and adds controls for titles, colors, backgrounds and opening behavior. Its Exsto menu is the clearest functional difference: long-press a folder to fan its apps into a circular launcher without first opening the folder.

Version 1.0.1 fixes small App Library icons shifting or being cut off when 4×5 folders or folder-icon position adjustments are active, and fixes the welcome card appearing tall and narrow on some devices, observed on iOS 14.

What LegacyFolder26 does better

LegacyFolder26 has a narrower goal and a more distinctive result: opening a folder produces a wide panel inspired by iOS 6. It offers Blur and “Frested Glass” appearances across its published range; Clear and Regular Liquid Glass are specifically labeled for iOS/iPadOS 26. A separate option removes the background blur behind the open folder.

Version 1.1.0 matters because the official changelog says it backports the tweak to iOS 15–18. Havoc now lists 15.0–26.0.1, replacing this page’s old iOS 26-only guidance. That remains a package range—not proof that every device has a jailbreak.

Licensing and privacy differences

Both are paid Havoc packages, but their published checks differ. LegacyFolder26 says it needs internet access approximately once a month for license verification.

PrimalFolderPro says activation sends the device UDID, model name and package identifier once over HTTPS to the developer’s server, which asks Havoc. The developer says the UDID is not stored; a hashed device ID, model and result may remain in logs for up to three days. The listing also says the tweak works for seven days after install or update, including offline. Read the current listing before purchasing in case these terms change.

Can you install both?

Neither listing documents PrimalFolderPro and LegacyFolder26 as a supported pairing. Because both modify SpringBoard folders, the cautious approach is to use one at a time. PrimalFolderPro explicitly says to remove PrimalFolder 2, Bolders Reborn and FolderX because it includes their functions; that warning does not name LegacyFolder26, so this article does not invent a confirmed conflict.

Before switching, note the current package and version, keep a working package manager, and know how to enter the jailbreak with tweak injection disabled. If SpringBoard starts looping, use the Dopamine bad-tweak recovery guide rather than repeatedly respringing.

Compatibility checklist before buying

  1. Confirm the exact device, iOS build and jailbreak—not just the Havoc package range.
  2. Confirm the package architecture matches the environment. PrimalFolderPro explicitly advertises rootful, rootless and RootHide; LegacyFolder26’s Havoc page confirms rootless support but does not make the same RootHide claim.
  3. Remove overlapping folder tweaks, or obtain explicit compatibility confirmation from both developers.
  4. Install dependencies only from configured official repositories.
  5. Keep a recovery path before enabling either SpringBoard tweak.

For firmware and architecture checks, use the rootless tweak compatibility checklist. Readers on iOS 26 should also check the Dopamine 3 device and firmware limits.

Verdict

PrimalFolderPro is the better all-round folder upgrade. Its dense layouts, alternate icon styles, title and color controls, and Exsto launcher change both appearance and workflow. The extra dollar buys a much wider feature set.

LegacyFolder26 is the better focused nostalgia tweak. It costs less and delivers the specific wide-panel design without pretending to be a full folder-management suite. Version 1.1.0 also makes it relevant beyond iOS 26 by adding official iOS 15–18 coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Which tweak supports more iOS versions?

Havoc lists PrimalFolderPro from iOS 14.0 and LegacyFolder26 from iOS 15.0; both listings currently end at 26.0.1. Actual jailbreak availability is narrower.

Does LegacyFolder26 support iOS 15–18 now?

Yes. The 1.1.0 changelog says it backports support to iOS 15–18, and Havoc now lists 15.0–26.0.1.

Does PrimalFolderPro support RootHide?

The developer’s official description explicitly lists rootful, rootless and RootHide environments. Compatibility still depends on the exact device, firmware and bootstrap.

Can either tweak provide a jailbreak?

No. These are SpringBoard tweaks installed after a compatible jailbreak and tweak-injection environment already exists.
