LockScreenRestore 1.9.4 Makes Music Artwork Tappable and Refines the iOS 15 Date Layout

The stable 1.9.4 release brings several visible iOS 15-style corrections to jailbroken iOS 16: tapping album artwork now opens the playing app, the date responds to Text Size, and the Focus pill is positioned and made interactive like the older Lock Screen.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Originally published September 28, 2026 · Updated October 7, 2026 · Stable release published October 3, 2026

What changed in LockScreenRestore 1.9.4?

The developer published the stable GitHub release on October 3, 2026, and Havoc listed it on October 4. Both official sources identify 1.9.4 as the current version and publish the same five release notes:

  • Music artwork now opens the playing app: tapping the large artwork follows iOS 15 behavior.
  • The date follows Text Size: it scales with the system text-size setting.
  • The date keeps the iOS 15 font: the release corrects a date that was too large on iPhone 8.
  • The Focus pill returns to the iOS 15 position: it sits 19 points below the date and can be tapped.
  • Dynamic Island remains a limitation: iPhone 14 Pro and newer Dynamic Island models are not optimized and may look wrong.

This is the stable follow-up to the 1.9.4 beta builds. It is not an expansion to iOS 17 or later, and it does not add RootHide or rootful packages.

What the tweak restores

LockScreenRestore is a free, open-source tweak intended to recreate the iOS 15 Lock Screen on iOS 16. It can restore the thin clock with its date below, the larger padlock, notifications directly under the clock, the iOS 15 music player, the Focus pill, the older unlock animation, Live and Dynamic wallpapers, Perspective Zoom, and the earlier Settings → Wallpaper page.

The features have separate switches, so users do not have to replace every iOS 16 element at once. That helps when another Lock Screen tweak overlaps with one component or when a particular device shape needs fewer changes.

Compatibility is narrower than “iOS 16” alone

Havoc lists iOS 16.0–16.7.16 and rootless support. The developer describes the project as iOS 16-only and tested with Dopamine. Its GitHub release asset is an iphoneos-arm64 rootless DEB with package identifier com.aronsz26.lockscreenrestore.

An iOS compatibility range does not mean every iPhone in that range has a jailbreak. The device and firmware must already be supported by a compatible rootless jailbreak. RootHide is a different packaging environment; users should not infer RootHide support from the word “rootless.”

The developer also says iPads are unsupported and Dynamic Island iPhones are not optimized. The tweak may install on a Dynamic Island model, but the layout can look off because it changes clock, padlock, date, notification and media-player geometry.

Who should update?

Users who rely on the restored music player get the clearest behavior change: album artwork becomes a shortcut to the playing app. People who increase or reduce system Text Size should see the date adapt without changing the clock itself, while iPhone 8 users receive the specific date-font correction named by the developer.

The Focus change is small but practical. A tappable pill restores both the placement and interaction expected from iOS 15 instead of only imitating its appearance. None of these changes promise broader jailbreak or device support.

How to update safely

  1. Refresh the official aronsz26 repository in Sileo or Zebra, or open the official Havoc listing.
  2. Confirm the package is com.aronsz26.lockscreenrestore, version 1.9.4, for a rootless environment.
  3. Make sure the device is on a supported iOS 16 firmware and already has a compatible rootless jailbreak.
  4. Install the update and respring when prompted.
  5. Test the date at the current Text Size setting, tap the Focus pill, and verify that music artwork opens the expected app.
  6. If the layout is wrong, disable overlapping Lock Screen tweaks first; on a Dynamic Island iPhone, remember the official optimization warning.

The GitHub release publishes a SHA-256 digest for its official DEB. Installing from the developer’s repository is simplest, but direct-download users can compare the asset digest with GitHub’s published value before opening it in Sileo.

Possible conflicts and limits

LockScreenRestore changes several SpringBoard surfaces. Tweaks that also replace the clock, move notifications, restyle the music player, alter the padlock or take over wallpaper behavior may conflict. Enable or disable one overlapping component at a time so the source of a layout problem remains clear.

The official notes do not claim iOS 17 or later support, iPad support, rootful support or RootHide support. They also do not claim Dynamic Island layout issues are fixed. Next Jailbreak verified the version, changelog, package scope and artwork against Havoc and GitHub; this article makes no hands-on testing claim.

Frequently asked questions

What is new in LockScreenRestore 1.9.4?

Music artwork opens the playing app, the date follows Text Size while keeping the iOS 15 font, and the Focus pill sits 19 points below the date and can be tapped.

Which iOS versions does Havoc list?

iOS 16.0 through 16.7.16 with a rootless jailbreak requirement. That package range does not create a jailbreak for every device in the range.

Does it support RootHide or rootful jailbreaks?

The official sources identify a rootless iphoneos-arm64 package and do not claim RootHide or rootful support.

Does it work on Dynamic Island iPhones?

The developer says it can be installed, but iPhone 14 Pro and newer Dynamic Island models are not optimized and the layout may look off.

Is LockScreenRestore free?

Yes. Havoc lists it as free, and the source code is published under the MIT license.

Before installing: confirm the current version, iOS range, package architecture and jailbreak environment at the official source for your exact device.
