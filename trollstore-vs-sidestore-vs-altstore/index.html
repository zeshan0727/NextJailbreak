TrollStore vs SideStore vs AltStore Classic: Which IPA Installer Should You Use?

TrollStore removes periodic signing only on a narrow set of firmware. SideStore covers iOS 14 and later with on-device refreshing. AltStore Classic 2.3 now adds Remote AltServer for installs and refreshes away from a computer, but raises its minimum to iOS 17.4. The best choice starts with your exact iOS version.

By Zeeshan Barvi · Published October 3, 2026 · Verified against current official releases

Quick answer

  • Choose TrollStore when your device is on an officially supported firmware and you want installed apps to remain signed without a seven-day refresh cycle.
  • Choose SideStore when you are on iOS 14 or later, accept Apple-ID development signing, and want installs and refreshes to run on the device after initial setup.
  • Choose AltStore Classic 2.3 on iOS 17.4 or later if you prefer AltStore’s sources and interface. Its new Remote AltServer can install, refresh, activate and deactivate apps from any Wi-Fi connection after setup.

TrollStore: permanent signing, narrow firmware window

opa334 describes TrollStore as a permasigned jailed app that can permanently install an IPA opened in it by using a CoreTrust bug. That is its decisive advantage: installed applications do not expire after the normal personal-development signing period.

The tradeoff is compatibility. Official support is limited to iOS and iPadOS 14.0 beta 2 through 16.6.1, iOS 16.7 RC build 20H18, and iOS 17.0. The project explicitly says other iOS 16.7.x releases and iOS 17.0.1 or newer will never be supported unless another suitable CoreTrust bug is found. A package claiming compatibility with a newer iOS version does not make TrollStore available there.

TrollStore is not a jailbreak. It can preserve powerful entitlements, but its README lists limits such as an inability to spawn launch daemons or inject tweaks into system processes. Use our TrollStore installation-method guide to match an official installer to your exact device and firmware, then follow the IPA installation guide.

SideStore: on-device signing from iOS 14 onward

SideStore is a community-driven AltStore fork for non-jailbroken devices. Its README says it signs apps with a personal development certificate, uses a specially designed VPN to enable installation on the device, and periodically refreshes apps before their normal seven-day development period expires.

The project lists iOS 14 or later as its requirement. Its current GitHub release is 0.7.0-alpha, published September 15, 2026. That release specifically addresses the Apple-server change that caused sign-in error 503 in SideStore 0.6.4 and earlier. Users still on an older build should not judge the current workflow by that fixed sign-in failure; see our SideStore 0.7.0-alpha update note.

SideStore’s main appeal is that normal installs and refreshes happen on-device after setup. It still uses Apple’s development-signing system, so “untethered” does not mean permanent signing and does not grant jailbreak capabilities.

AltStore Classic 2.3: Remote AltServer changes the old comparison

AltStore Classic also signs apps with your Apple ID. Historically, AltStore had to find AltServer on the same network to install or refresh apps. Version 2.3, published September 30, 2026, adds Remote AltServer: the official changelog says it can install, refresh, activate and deactivate apps from anywhere with a Wi-Fi connection and supports sideloading without a computer after setup.

That update makes older comparisons that call AltStore permanently computer-tethered incomplete. However, the official AltStore download page still says AltStore Classic requires AltServer to install, and version 2.3 raises the minimum supported iOS version to 17.4. SideStore therefore retains a compatibility advantage on iOS 14 through 17.3.

Do not confuse AltStore Classic with AltStore PAL. PAL is a separate marketplace product available only in listed regions; this comparison concerns Classic, the Apple-ID sideloading app.

What the seven-day rule means

SideStore and AltStore Classic use personal development signing rather than TrollStore’s CoreTrust path. Their official project descriptions both discuss refreshing applications to prevent expiration. Keep the relevant refresh mechanism working and refresh before the signing period ends. A paid Apple Developer membership changes provisioning terms, but it does not turn either app into TrollStore or a jailbreak.

TrollStore avoids this refresh cycle on supported firmware, which is why it remains the simplest option when compatibility lines up. The security cost is also worth remembering: powerful or broadly entitled IPA files should come only from the original developer.

Which one should you choose?

Your device is on iOS 17.0 or earlier

Check TrollStore compatibility first. If your exact build is supported, permanent signing is usually the strongest practical advantage. If it is not supported but you are on iOS 14 or later, SideStore is the broader current option.

Your device is on iOS 17.4 or newer

TrollStore is not officially available. Choose between SideStore and AltStore Classic based on workflow: SideStore remains focused on on-device operation, while AltStore Classic 2.3 now offers Remote AltServer plus AltStore’s source ecosystem.

You need jailbreak tweaks or system injection

None of these three is a jailbreak. Installing an IPA does not provide tweak injection, kernel privileges or support for jailbreak-only packages. Check a real jailbreak’s device and firmware requirements separately.

Safety checklist

  • Download the installer and IPA only from the developer’s official site, repository or release page.
  • Confirm your exact iOS build; a broad “iOS 17 compatible” claim is not enough for TrollStore.
  • Expect Apple-ID-signed SideStore and AltStore apps to require refreshes.
  • Do not assume a sideloading tool removes the need for a jailbreak when an app explicitly requires one.
  • Back up app data before switching signing tools because bundle identity and provisioning changes can affect an existing installation.
