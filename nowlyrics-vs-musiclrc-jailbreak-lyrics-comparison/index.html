NowLyrics vs MusicLRC: Which Jailbreak Lyrics Tweak Should You Use?

Choose NowLyrics when you want synchronized lyrics across the Lock Screen, Control Center, Dynamic Island, CarPlay and multiple streaming apps. Choose MusicLRC when your library consists of local Apple Music files with LRC or TTML lyrics already stored in their tags.

By Zeeshan BarviPublished October 19, 20267 min read
Short answer: these are not two versions of the same idea. NowLyrics 1.2.1 is the broader, paid system-playback tool with online sources, imports and word-by-word Apple Music lyrics. MusicLRC 1.1 is the focused, free option for tagged local songs inside Apple Music.

The decisive difference: where the lyrics come from

NowLyrics searches, imports and manages lyric sources. Its original release supports LRCLIB and NetEase without an account and Musixmatch with the user’s token or API key. Version 1.2.0 added Apple Music’s own lyrics, including word-by-word timing where Apple supplies it; a subscription is required for that source. The tweak can also paste or import LRC, word-by-word LRC and TTML files, and lets manually added lyrics override online results.

MusicLRC expects the work to be done in the audio file. Its official description says local song files must already contain LRC or TTML lyrics in their tags. It is not presented as an online search service, lyric downloader or streaming-catalog extension. If a local track has no correctly timed tagged data, installing MusicLRC alone does not create it.

This makes the choice unusually clear: users who want automatic lookup and broad playback coverage should start with NowLyrics; users maintaining their own local, tagged library may prefer MusicLRC’s narrower model.

Where each tweak displays lyrics

NowLyrics originally replaced the track-title field with the line being sung on the Lock Screen and in Control Center, the Dynamic Island and CarPlay. Its current settings let users control the artist field, line offset, lyric source order and selected music apps. Version 1.2.0 also added a word-by-word Dynamic Island presentation and a Now Playing management page for source selection and song-specific offsets.

MusicLRC operates inside Apple Music. Its official screenshot shows the same local track before and after the tweak: raw timestamped lines become a large, centered, synchronized lyric display. That is valuable for local collections, but the official source does not claim Lock Screen, Control Center, Dynamic Island, CarPlay or third-party player support.

NowLyrics offers far more control

NowLyrics 1.2.0 was a major expansion rather than a small visual refresh. It introduced source ordering, a searchable lyric library, per-song offsets, imported lyrics, Apple Music and Musixmatch word timing, player and Dynamic Island previews, and integrations that display NextUp 3’s queue row and Crescendo’s volume slider inside previews.

Version 1.2.1 then focused on efficiency and correctness. Its official changelog says settings pages became faster and battery use was reduced. It fixed a half-completed back swipe leaving the preview player open, restored touch interaction for the NextUp 3 and Crescendo preview controls, corrected removal of manually added lyrics, stopped unnecessary Now Playing redraws, lowered memory use and stopped the waveform animation when its page is not active.

MusicLRC deliberately exposes a smaller feature surface. The official listing documents its tagged-file behavior rather than source selection, offsets, caches or alternate music players. For someone who already prepares local files correctly, that focus can be an advantage rather than a deficiency.

Compatibility: package ranges are not jailbreak ranges

Havoc lists NowLyrics 1.2.1 for iOS 15.0 through 26.0.1 and marks it as rootless. Havoc lists MusicLRC 1.1 for iOS 13.0 through 18.7.9 and also marks it as rootless. MusicLRC’s developer additionally warns that the internal implementation is complex and may be broken on some versions; iOS 26 is described only as possible future support.

Those are package-compatibility claims, not a statement that every device on every listed version can be jailbroken. Your exact iPhone or iPad, firmware, bootstrap and tweak-injection environment still need to be supported. Neither public listing checked here explicitly promises a RootHide build, so do not treat “Supports Rootless” as RootHide compatibility.

NowLyrics’ broad upper range makes it the only documented choice between these two for iOS 26. MusicLRC’s lower minimum makes it the only listed choice for iOS 13 and 14. On overlapping iOS 15–18 versions, the music source and desired display surface should decide.

What changed in MusicLRC 1.1?

MusicLRC 1.1 was released on October 17, 2026. The official changelog adds iOS 13 support, confirms iOS 14 support, fixes operation on iOS 14.0–14.2 while Airplane Mode is enabled, and includes patchfinder optimizations. The listing still cautions that some OS versions may not behave correctly.

The version is important because an earlier site entry described MusicLRC 1.0 while noting conflicting source records. The live official Havoc page now clearly lists 1.1, so this comparison uses 1.1 throughout and does not repeat the older version as current.

Can both tweaks be installed together?

The official pages checked do not document coexistence. Both can affect lyric behavior in Apple Music, though they use different input models. Installing both may create overlapping hooks or an unclear troubleshooting path. Use one at a time unless the developers explicitly confirm compatibility.

If you change tools, uninstall the first package through Sileo or Zebra, complete the requested restart action, then install the other from Havoc. Do not delete tweak files manually. The safe Sileo uninstall guide explains how to review the removal queue.

Verdict

  • Choose NowLyrics 1.2.1 for Spotify or other streaming apps, online lyric sources, Apple Music word-by-word lyrics, imported files, the Dynamic Island, Lock Screen, Control Center or CarPlay.
  • Choose MusicLRC 1.1 for a local Apple Music collection whose files already contain correctly timed LRC or TTML tags, especially when a free and focused tool is preferable.
  • Choose neither yet if you have not confirmed the exact jailbreak environment. A listed iOS range alone is not enough.

NowLyrics is the more capable product. MusicLRC is the more specialized one. The best choice follows the source of your music: streamed and system-wide playback favors NowLyrics; curated local files favor MusicLRC.

Frequently asked questions

Does MusicLRC download lyrics?

The official listing does not claim lyric downloading. It says LRC or TTML lyrics must already be saved in the local song’s tags.

Does NowLyrics support local lyric files?

Yes. The current official description supports pasted or imported LRC, word-by-word LRC and TTML data, and manually added lyrics take priority over online sources.

Which tweak supports iOS 26?

Havoc lists NowLyrics through iOS 26.0.1. MusicLRC is listed only through iOS 18.7.9 and says iOS 26 support may be added in the future.

Does either listing guarantee RootHide support?

No. Both are marked as supporting rootless packaging, but the official pages checked do not explicitly promise RootHide builds.
