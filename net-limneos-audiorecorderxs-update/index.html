AudioRecorder XS Adds Ringtone Support and a Built-In Audio Editor

The official AudioRecorder XS package page highlights two headline additions: the ability to set recordings as ringtones directly on the device and a high-performance audio editor. The tweak is described as supporting iOS 12.0 through 27.0 and recording audio directly on the iPhone rather than relying on a remote recording server. It also lists support for calls and audio from multiple CallKit services, voice memos, system audio, cloud synchronization, sharing, automation, and playback tools. However, the page contains mixed range wording—its title says “iOS 12 to 26,” while the compatibility text says “iOS 12.0 to 27.0”—so users should confirm the applicable support range before installation.

Key points

  • The package page promotes Set Ringtone support and a high-performance Audio Editor as the newest headline capabilities.
  • The listed compatibility text says iOS 12.0 through 27.0, although the page title says “iOS 12 to 26.”
  • The tweak is described as recording directly on the device, without a remote server or an internet connection for telephony recording.
  • Supported recording categories listed by the developer include regular phone calls, CallKit services, voice memos, and system audio.
  • Users can edit recordings, share or export them, synchronize with Dropbox or Google Drive, and manage recording automation.
  • Call recording laws differ by location; the developer places responsibility for notification, consent, and lawful use on the user.

What the AudioRecorder XS page highlights

AudioRecorder XS is presented as an on-device recording tweak for iPhones. The package page calls out two prominent additions: a “Set Ringtone” ability and a high-performance Audio Editor. Those additions extend the package beyond basic capture by allowing users to process recordings and use them as ringtones directly on the device.

The page also lists AudioRecorder XS as supporting iOS 12.0 to 27.0. That statement needs careful reading because the page heading separately uses the wording “iOS 12 to 26.” Since both ranges appear in the same source, prospective users should treat the exact upper-version support as something to confirm before purchasing or installing.

  • Headline additions: Set Ringtone support and a high-performance Audio Editor.
  • The page identifies AudioRecorder XS as the iOS 12-and-newer successor to AudioRecorder 2 for iOS 8–11.
  • The listed price is $3.99.

Recording coverage extends beyond cellular calls

The developer describes AudioRecorder XS as capable of recording regular phone calls directly on an iPhone. The feature list also names calls from multiple CallKit services, including FaceTime, Skype, Viber, Messenger, Tango, LINE, BOTIM, and Instagram. The page separately refers to FaceTime audio and video calls, with the audio portion recorded.

The package can also record voice memos from the device microphone, with instant activation actions, and capture system audio—described as sounds coming out of the device, or “what you hear.” The source gives examples such as songs and website audio. These are distinct recording modes, so users should review the available application-specific settings and activation methods for the workflow they need.

  • Record telephony calls and calls from the listed CallKit services.
  • Capture microphone-based voice memos.
  • Record system audio from sounds played by the device.
  • Configure automation per application and assign contacts whose calls should always be recorded.

Editing, playback, and ringtone workflow

The built-in editor is described as supporting common post-recording operations. The listed tools include cutting, inserting silence, and applying effects such as reverb, equalization, and fade-in or fade-out. The page also promotes an improved player and a visualizer for playback.

After editing, the new Set Ringtone capability is intended to let users use recordings as ringtones natively on the device. The source does not specify limits such as maximum ringtone length, supported export formats, or whether every recording type can be assigned as a ringtone. Those details should be checked in the package’s current documentation or interface before relying on a particular workflow.

  • Cut recordings and insert silence.
  • Apply reverb, equalizer, and fade-in/fade-out effects.
  • Use the improved player and recording visualizer.
  • Set recordings as ringtones on the device.

Storage, sharing, and transfer options

AudioRecorder XS is described as storing recordings locally on the iPhone. The developer says the tweak does not use a remote server to record calls and that an internet connection is not required when recording a telephony call. This is different from later sharing or backup actions, which may require access to the relevant services or applications.

The listed export options include email, iMessage, Dropbox, Google Drive, and other supported applications on the device. Users can also share multiple recordings at once by placing them in a ZIP archive. Automatic synchronization to Dropbox or Google Drive is included in the feature list, while a web-browser option is provided for copying calls from the phone to a computer.

  • Keep recordings locally on the iPhone.
  • Automatically synchronize recordings with Dropbox or Google Drive.
  • Send recordings through email, iMessage, or supported apps.
  • ZIP and share multiple recordings together.
  • Use a web browser to copy calls to a computer.

Activation and automation controls

The package page says users can assign activation methods to bring up the recorder interface. It also lists automation controls per application and the ability to assign contacts for automatic recording. These options are aimed at reducing the need to start recording manually for every supported call.

The source does not enumerate every activation method or explain how automation behaves across each named service. Before enabling automatic recording, users should check the available settings and make sure the behavior matches local consent requirements and their intended use.

  • Assign activation methods for opening the recorder interface.
  • Manage recording automation separately by application.
  • Mark selected contacts for automatic call recording.

Compatibility and installation checks

The official page provides a broad compatibility statement but uses inconsistent range wording. Its title says “AudioRecorder XS (iOS 12 to 26),” while the body says “Supports iOS 12.0 to 27.0.” The material supplied here does not include a package version, a dated changelog, a list of tested devices, or a detailed dependency list. As a result, users should not infer support for a particular point release or device solely from the headline.

Before installing, check the package manager’s current compatibility display, the developer’s current package page, and any jailbreak or system requirements shown there. Users should also back up important recordings before changing package settings, migrating devices, or removing the tweak. The source confirms local storage as a feature, but it does not promise recovery after an uninstall or system change.

  • Resolve the iOS 26-versus-27 wording before installation.
  • Check the current package metadata and device requirements.
  • Do not assume a specific point release or hardware model is supported without confirmation.
  • Back up recordings before making system or package changes.

Call-recording consent remains the user’s responsibility

The developer includes a legal disclaimer stating that recording conversations without the other party’s consent is illegal in many countries. AudioRecorder XS can automatically play a warning voice message at the beginning of a recording, but the page says users are responsible for enabling or disabling that option according to local law and for ensuring the other party is notified.

The disclaimer also warns that a notification may fail to play or may not be heard because of issues such as a bad signal. In those circumstances, the developer says responsibility for notification and consent remains with the user. Laws can differ between the countries or jurisdictions of the participants, so users should obtain appropriate legal guidance when necessary and use the recording features only lawfully.

  • Review the laws that apply to everyone involved in a call.
  • Enable the warning message where appropriate, but do not treat it as a guarantee of legal compliance.
  • Obtain consent when required and avoid assuming that a failed notification removes responsibility.
