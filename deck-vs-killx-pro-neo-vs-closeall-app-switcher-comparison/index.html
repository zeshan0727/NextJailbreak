Deck 1.2.0 vs KillX Pro Neo 3.0.1 vs CloseAll 1.0: Which App Switcher Tweak Fits?

Deck replaces the App Switcher experience, KillX Pro Neo adds a polished cleaner to the stock layout, and CloseAll covers the essential safety controls for free. The right choice depends more on scope and firmware than feature count.

Published November 25, 2026Live package data checked
Quick answer: choose Deck 1.2.0 for grid or carousel layouts and integrated Control Center tools; choose KillX Pro Neo 3.0.1 if you want the stock switcher with a refined close-all gesture, app locks, music protection and haptics; choose CloseAll 1.0 for the free, minimal option. Do not install them together.

Choose Deck when the layout is the feature

Deck is the only option here that turns the App Switcher into a different workspace. It offers grid and carousel layouts, selectable card sizes and shapes, per-card close controls, pinned apps, an app count and Close All. Its original layouts can place Control Center cards below the app cards or use a compact control bar.

Version 1.2.0 adds drag-to-pin, optional protection for the app playing audio, quick actions and a Side by Side page. On iOS 18, that page can show Apple’s own Control Center modules and open their full controls with a long press. The official Chariz index publishes both rootful and rootless builds, each requiring MobileSubstrate, PreferenceLoader and iOS 15 or later. Read the Deck 1.2.0 update guide for the detailed layout choices.

Choose KillX Pro Neo for stock-switcher cleanup

KillX Pro Neo does not try to replace every card or control. Its main workflow is one downward swipe on an App Switcher card to close the remaining apps, while locked cards stay open. Version 3.0.1 fixes the long-press lock gesture accidentally triggering a tap action; the current Havoc page dates that fix to September 17, 2026.

It also recognizes the app currently playing media, marks it with a music glyph and protects it from closing. Permanent locks, kill animations, button styles and distinct haptic responses make it the more configurable cleaner. The important limitation is device support: Havoc explicitly says it is not compatible with iPadOS or tweaks that force iPadOS features.

Choose CloseAll for the free essentials

CloseAll is free and open source. It adds a Close button at the top-right of the stock App Switcher, lets you swipe down on a card to lock or unlock it, and automatically locks Now Playing apps. Its official description says there are no options to configure and no dependencies, which makes it the simplest choice.

There is one compatibility discrepancy users should not ignore: the page tagline says “iOS 15–26,” but Havoc’s live structured compatibility field currently stops at iOS 18.7.5. Until the developer updates or confirms the formal range, treat 18.7.5 as the conservative maximum. The listing also says CloseAll is not compatible with Grid Switcher.

Which one should you install?

  • Deck: you want a visual overhaul, multiple layouts, integrated controls or iPad support, and your device is on iOS 15–18.7.
  • KillX Pro Neo: you use an iPhone on a compatible rootless jailbreak and want the safest, most polished cleaner without replacing the switcher layout.
  • CloseAll: you want a free Close button and basic locking, accept the lack of preferences and are within the formally listed iOS 15–18.7.5 range.
  • None: your jailbreak already provides the behavior you need, or your exact device, firmware and environment are not supported.

An iOS compatibility range describes the package, not jailbreak availability. Verify the exact device, chip, firmware and jailbreak first with the jailbreak tweak compatibility checklist.

Safe installation and conflict recovery

  1. Install only one App Switcher modifier. Deck, KillX Pro Neo and CloseAll all touch card gestures, locking or mass-closing behavior.
  2. Use the official Chariz or Havoc listing; do not use repackaged paid DEBs.
  3. Record your existing switcher tweaks and pin settings. Follow the safe Sileo installation guide, respring, then test app switching, app locking, media playback and Close All.
  4. Do not judge protection with an unsaved draft or active transfer. Closing an app can still discard state even when the tweak itself works correctly.
  5. If cards disappear, gestures conflict or SpringBoard crashes, boot with tweak injection disabled, remove the newest switcher tweak and respring. Use the Dopamine bad-tweak recovery guide for the safe sequence.
